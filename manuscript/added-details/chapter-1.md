# Chapter 1: Added Details not in Scene Packet

Source for the appendix of each scene PDF. One "## Scene N (Draft D)" section per scene, with "### " sub-headings for parts of the scene and "- " bullets for each detail. Details that the author's later decisions made canon are marked "(decided)" with where they are recorded.

## Scene 1 (Draft 2)

### Arrival and the castle
- Alexander rode out at seven, sent by Edward to look at the second cut on Harrow's Fen, and comes back up the hill road at about eleven. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- The rooks in the chestnuts above the gate are louder than the fiddle. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- The hay ricks on the Fen stand "like loaves set out to cool," and a sluice gate there wants seeing to.
- Alexander looks at a house "from the oldest part outward," his own included.
- The keep was "built by men who had not expected to be visited."
- The castle stone has weathered to the colour of an old hayrick.
- Red and yellow harvest ribbons are tied to the old iron rings along the gate wall.
- Alexander gives his horse and his coat to a stable boy at the gate.

### The cider cart
- The cart is stuck just inside the gate, where the ground is always soft and somebody forgets every year.
- Mr. Fenwick is short and square, with a voice made for calling cattle across three fields. He says "Hold hard, bor."
- Carrow is one of the four hands: the youngest, wiry and quick, the Widow Carrow's son, the fastest rider in the stables. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- Fenwick puts his hat back on "in order to take it off again."
- Alexander is the tallest by half a head and the only one with soft hands. He has a splinter in the heel of his right hand and cider on his charcoal waistcoat.
- His loose cravat is "a rebellion small enough to deny."
- The Widow Carrow, in her Sunday black, tells him: "You've his eyes, Master Alexander. Always looking for what's wrong with a thing." He doesn't believe her.

### The judging table
- Four marrows. Hodge is tall and stooped and "argued with his whole neck."
- Edward's left hand turns something in his pocket when he listens (the rifle ball, unnamed). (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- Edward checks his watch during the judging.
- Edward: "That is a tradition. It is not an argument." and "Whether the marrow is large, and whether it is honest."
- The Widow Carrow's entry is second from the left.
- Edward gives the marrow the pause he gives letters arriving under seal from London.

### Amelia
- She stands at the end of the preserves table, in a straw bonnet.
- She takes off a glove to taste Mrs. Davies's damson cheese.
- The winter Alexander was sixteen she never took her gloves off and said it was the cold, and he teased her until she laughed. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- "Let him have his war. It's the only one I allow him."

### Thomas Barrow and Harrow's Fen
- Thomas holds his tally book "the way other men held a prayer book."
- Extra cider is coming from Thrower's.
- Thomas's father brought the last load of hay up himself, on his bad knee. "Most estates, a man goes lame, he goes without wage the same week. Not here." (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- The Davies boys fight over the swing on the lowest chestnut bough.
- The Widow Carrow keeps one cow.
- Alexander counts seven of the eleven families before losing track.

### The kitchen
- Mrs. Thompson runs four girls and a boy with a wooden spoon. Festival ribbons hang from the drying rack.
- She plucks a straw from his hair: "Shows up something terrible on black, that does, love."
- Catherine wears figured blue silk with a muddied hem.
- Catherine says "Master Alexander" and "You look like a portrait of a duke that someone has left out in the rain."
- Alexander: "And you have brought the drawing room into the kitchen."
- Catherine's Saxony harvest crown, made from the last sheaf.
- Alexander eats the tart standing, as he hasn't in a kitchen since he was ten. The window shows the road toward Norwich.

## Scene 2 (Draft 1)

### The yard
- At two o'clock the festival sound includes a drum and somebody's dog.
- Niall's coat is folded on the bench. "So the kitchen tells me."
- The box holds five knives. Alexander kept the sixth, from his first good throw, wrapped in Niall's pencil diagram, and Niall never replaced it. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- Niall's hair is "the colour of a fox's back." His notebook rides in his waistcoat pocket.
- The throwing line is at seven paces, which Niall paced out in the first spring. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- The painted rings on the chestnut have thickened with repainting "like the rings of the tree."

### The lesson
- "Your wrist, Alex" is the book's first use of the name. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- Niall's thumb moves off the splinter wound without comment. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- In the first year Niall corrected him with the flat of another knife, never his hand (from the prequel).
- "Better," which was the most he ever said.
- Niall's eyes are "a pale and ordinary blue" until they go still.
- The rooks go up out of the far trees twice. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- "Four hundred and ten yards." "I didn't ask how far." "You were going to."
- The forest "hasn't moved in living memory... a point of family pride."

### Catherine
- She comes through the gate from the kitchen garden.
- Niall says "Sir," then "Lady Catherine." She hears the change. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five)
- "Don't stop on my account, Alex."
- The handkerchiefs are white lawn, each with a blue swallow, tail spread.
- Niall folds his in four into the notebook. (decided: Gap Resolutions, Part Five) He says "Thank you, my lady."
- "Don't let him throw at Mr. Hodge, however much he deserves it."
- Alexander lays the last knife pointing the way Niall always points them.
- Closing: the clouds come up out of the west.

## Scene 3 (Draft 1)

### The steps
- The family waits on the front steps to receive their own dinner guests.
- Alexander is still in his riding coat, brushed by the stable boy.
- Catherine wears dark green and sits on the balustrade until Amelia tells her to get off it.
- The Reverend Mr. Ashby is always first to dinner, comes in a noisy carriage with a loose wheel-band, and drives a blowing cob.

### The carriage
- The rooks go up "with a noise like a sheet being torn."
- The door panel is plain black lacquer, "as blank as a closed eye." The carriage is "as shut as a box."
- The stable horses are unsettled while Hessian's pair stand dead still.
- The coachman's eyes rest "on the air between his horses' ears." He does not get down, and the door opens anyway.

### Hessian
- His coat "might have been cut ten years ago by the best tailor in Vienna." His accent is too faint to place.
- His smile is one Alexander "had seen before, on someone else."
- "Forgive me. I am early." "For everything, I find."
- His title is "from the Emperor," and he says they need not use it. "I have come to you about land."
- He knows the name of Mr. Hollis, the family's man of business.
- "Land is never tedious. It is only waiting to be wanted."
- A cold breath of air comes with him, and the lantern flame leans away.
- The letter's black seal carries no device.
- He bows over Amelia's hand without touching it.
- He looks Alexander over "from the oldest part outward."

### The turn
- Catherine once followed a German tinker halfway to Swaffham to ask him about the Rhine.
- Amelia's gloved hand rests on Catherine's arm.
- "No one is turned from the door tonight." "I accept what I am given."
- A footman says "my lord," and Alexander hears it as a warning for the first time.
- From the landing window the chestnuts are empty, and Hessian laughs with Mr. Ashby.

## Scene 4 (Draft 1)

### The hall
- Sheaves in the corners, wheat and red ribbon on the gallery rail, Hodge's marrow on the high table "like a bishop."
- Guests: the Reverend Mr. and Mrs. Ashby, the Fenwicks, the Hodges, the Lowndes from Holt, Thomas Barrow.
- Hessian sits at Amelia's right, and Catherine across from him.
- Seraphina walked over before the lamps were lit and spent the hour before dinner upstairs with Amelia.
- When Alexander was six, Seraphina looked into a basin of water and told him he would be tall.
- O'Brien is "pressed as sharp as a new card."

### The talk
- Amelia's correspondents are in Vienna, in Pest, and in Ragusa (an old gentleman who writes only about his figs). Catherine mentions a lantern procession to the water.
- Hessian: "I was born above it." He describes the Iron Gates, whirlpools, a fortress on a crag, and blue doors against the evil eye.
- He moves his goose once with his fork, eats nothing, and turns his glass a quarter turn.
- Catherine: "He powders... vain as a curate." "I never embarrass anyone. I'm a delight."
- Seraphina's eye clouds "like a pane of glass when someone breathes on it." Alexander has seen it before, at christenings, funerals, and once in a storm.
- The vicar has a limp.
- Mr. Ashby's speech mentions the Euphrates in Revelation and runs twenty minutes.

### Edward's land
- Edward: "Wester Ross. Near Loch Maree. Rock and heather and a good deal of weather."
- Edward's grandfather said it was the only property that never gave him trouble.
- Hessian: "It is a foreign eccentricity."
- Hessian leaves by the door where the draught comes in. His wine is untouched, with no mark on the rim.

## Scene 5 (Draft 1)

### The garden
- Alexander goes down the back stair in an old coat because he cannot get warm.
- Someone is still scouring pans in the kitchen. A single candle burns in the long gallery.
- Niall stands among the cabbages: "They're quiet company. Unlike some."
- The shape on the wall is forty yards off and sounds "like a heavy cloak thrown open."
- Niall: "Stay there, Alex."
- Alexander turns his ankle on a bed edge. Beyond the wall is a paddock, and a drunk man is singing on the hill road.
- Niall is gone about five minutes.
- The feather is barred dark brown, longer than Alexander's forearm, with a quill as thick as a pen.
- Edward once took Alexander to the brecks near Thetford to see the last great bustards in Norfolk.
- Niall pockets the feather with the same movement as Edward pocketing the letter.
- "Go up. You'll take cold." "I've been cold since six o'clock."

### The gallery
- Catherine sets one candle on the sill and sits in the window seat in green silk. The clock is on the landing.
- She has the Iron Gates on her atlas page "in a thin blue thread."
- Every ancestor portrait was painted indoors. She noticed it at twelve.
- Great-grandmamma Harriet never left Norfolk, is painted in grey with a prayer book, and hangs by the door.
- She hears a man's voice in the garden.
- The gallery boards sing under everyone else, and Alexander crosses them "like a herald."
- The house was built by four Sterlings who disliked each other.
- Catherine's future: "very nice rooms," choosing the curtains, a portrait with a spaniel.
- The candle flame stands straight; there is no draught.
- "Like the first notes of a piece she had wanted all her life to be able to play."
- Hessian will call on Edward on Tuesday "about a little wet meadow I saw from the road."
- "He'd sooner sell me." Hessian does not laugh.
- Catherine does not hear his steps leaving.
