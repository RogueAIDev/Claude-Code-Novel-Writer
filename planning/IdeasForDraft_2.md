# Ideas for Draft 2: Side Plots and Inner Lives for *Inheritance*
### Chronicles of the Shadow Vanguard, Book One

**Status: PROPOSALS ONLY.** Nothing in this file is canon until the author marks an item **ADOPTED**. Drafting agents must not use any item here unless it is marked ADOPTED, and every adopted item is also copied into `Gap_Resolutions.md`.

This file collects every side-plot and character suggestion made so far, held here for now. The ten suggestions from the first round all appear below under new codes: Alexander's Grey Year investigation (B1), Niall's monthly errand (C1), the coachman's wrists (M1), Catherine at the door (M3), Higgins hesitates (M2), the boy in the cage (L6), the Fen families at the gate (L1), Bess at the gate (L3), and the face in the refuge (M4). The tenth, why Hessian chose Catherine, is answered by canon: she resembles Elisabeta because she descends from her (Main Character Bible, Hessian's Secrets).

Each idea grows out of something the bibles already fix about a character: a want, a fear, a habit, or a secret. The aim is not more plot. It is to make every character someone with a life that goes on when Alexander isn't looking at them. Each item gives:

- **Where:** the scenes it touches, in the outline's numbering
- **Adds:** what it does for the reader
- **Canon:** what it rests on, and anything it must not contradict
- **Cost:** how much page it takes

Items marked **YOUR CALL** would fix something for later books, so they need the author's decision before they are used.

## PART ONE: WHAT MAKES A CHARACTER FEEL REAL

A reader believes in a character who wants something besides the main plot, keeps at least one private habit, hides at least one thing, can be funny, and has one relationship whose stakes have nothing to do with the plot. The bibles already give every principal most of these. The table shows the private life each one is carrying, so the draft can let it show.

| Character | What they want that isn't the plot | What they do alone | What they hide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alexander** | To be close to someone without waiting for permission | Throws knives alone when he can't sleep; keeps a commonplace book | That he loves Niall; that Will Pryce still shapes him |
| **Niall** | A life beside Alexander that no one assigned him | Writes Gaelic in his father's notebook; works the cipher at night; oils his knives | The money to Calum's mother and to Isla; the rib scar; the 1848 meeting |
| **Catherine** | Every route in the atlas | Reads Lady Hester Stanhope; embroiders swallows; dances | The pin money; what she has seen in Alexander's face; Calum's feelings |
| **Edward** | An orderly handover at twenty-five | Questions footmen in the pantry on things that aren't his business | His brother's death; the rifle ball |
| **Amelia** | To teach Catherine magic openly | Scrying at Seraphina's cottage; writing to foreign contacts | The lost pregnancies; a private record of all the family doesn't say |
| **Calum** | A bindery of his own | Mends books wherever he finds them | His feeling for Catherine |
| **Seraphina** | Amelia's forgiveness | Walks; keeps herbs; scries | That she once loved someone in the Circle |
| **Beatrice** | A Vanguard that loses no one to procedure | Keeps meticulous records | She bends protocol for people who prove themselves |
| **Percival** | To beat his father | Retells his own legend | The truth of the scar |

## PART TWO: ALEXANDER AND NIALL — THE LOVE NEITHER WILL RISK

The bible fixes this: both men love each other, both believe the love is unreturned or impossible, and in Book One neither says it. What makes it believable is not the longing. It is that each has a concrete reason to stay silent, and the reasons are different.

- **Alexander's reason:** he fears that Niall's friendship was an assignment. He has never learned how to reach for anyone, and he has been taught by Will Pryce's disappearance that the people he loves can be removed by decisions made over his head.
- **Niall's reason:** he is an employee of the house. If his feelings were known, he would be sent away, and he would have damaged Alexander's future. Rank stands between them, and after 17.4 it gets worse: Alexander becomes his employer.

**A1. The friendship was an order (4.4, 5.6).**
In the prequel, Niall refused to teach Alexander knife throwing, and Edward overruled him. When the truth comes out at 4.4, Alexander's oldest fear turns out to be technically true: the lessons were an order. He cannot make himself ask, "Were you told to be my friend?" So he asks something next to it, about the lessons, and Niall, who knows exactly what he is being asked, answers the question underneath: "I was ordered to teach you to throw. Nobody ordered the rest." That line could carry the promise at 5.6, or come just before it.
- *Adds:* It gives the reader the real wound beneath Plot Turn One, and makes "I'll not decide for you again what you're fit to carry" an answer to a fear, not just a promise.
- *Canon:* Prequel Chapter 7 (Edward overrules Niall); both men's fears in the Main Character Bible.
- *Cost:* One exchange.

**A2. Near-misses, each swerving differently (3.4, 13.2, 19.6, 26.5).**
Book One has four moments where one of them comes to the edge of saying it. Make each swerve take a different form, so the reader feels a pattern rather than a repetition:
- **3.4, February, in the library.** Swerved by a practical task: Niall answers honestly about Catherine and then makes up the fire.
- **13.2, "loved."** Swerved by silence: the word is said about Calum, and Alexander hears it land somewhere else.
- **19.6, "I'll follow it."** Swerved by duty: the closest Niall can come is a vow of service.
- **26.5, the handkerchief.** Swerved by a choice: each sees what the other saw, and both choose not to speak.
- *Adds:* Rising tension without a confession, which the bible forbids until Book Six.
- *Cost:* None. This is a drafting instruction for scenes already in the outline.

**A3. "His Grace's man" (Chapters 19–26).**
After Edward's death, someone (Beatrice, O'Brien, or Sebastian) refers to Niall as "His Grace's man," meaning Alexander's. Both men flinch, for different reasons. The imbalance that makes confession impossible has just doubled. 26.5, "beside me, not behind me," is the answer to that moment.
- *Adds:* Makes the ending earned rather than sentimental.
- *Cost:* One line, then the payoff already in the outline.

**A4. The knife lessons as their only language (1.2, 6.3, 6.6, 26.5).**
The only time they touch freely is when Niall corrects Alexander's grip. Let the draft track it. In 1.2 the correction is easy and long familiar. After 4.4 the first correction is stiff, and Niall's hand leaves too soon. By Chapter 26 Alexander corrects Niall, once.
- *Cost:* Drafting instruction only.

**A5. Catherine knows, and protects it (1.2, 20.3, 25.7).**
Catherine's bible lists among her secrets "what she has seen in Alexander's face when he looks at Niall." She never says it in Book One. Once, late (20.3 or 25.7), she comes to the edge of saying it and chooses not to, which is her own small act of protection. Alexander sees her choose, and does not know what she was about to say.
- *Canon:* Catherine's Secrets field. Must stay unspoken in Book One.

**A6. The shadow of the law (3.5). YOUR CALL.**
In London, over breakfast, a newspaper carries a short item about a man convicted under the laws against what Alexander feels. Alexander turns the page. Nobody at the table remarks on it. The style guide says the law should be a pressure, never a lecture, and that everyone already knows it, so this is a single turned page.
- *Adds:* The romance's danger made concrete, once, early.
- *Your call:* It is the only place Book One would name the legal threat on the page, even obliquely.

## PART THREE: SIDE PLOTS FOR THE MAIN CAST

### Alexander

**B1. His own investigation in the Grey Year (Chapter 3).**
Give Alexander one lead of his own: the buyer of the northern tract. He finds Ivor Vane's chambers. There is a brass plate, a clerk who knows nothing, and rates paid by banker's draft from an account nobody can name. Pike has been there the day before.
- *Adds:* Alexander is active in the book's most passive stretch, the reader gets an early chill about the Count, and Pike's secret investigation is planted from the start, as the author's ruling has it.
- *Canon:* The Supporting Bible names Vane's firm as "the loose thread" Pike won't officially pull. Amelia's foreign-suitor story to Pike (3.1) already points toward a foreign gentleman.
- *Cost:* A half-scene in 3.4 or 3.5.

**B2. Three in the morning (Chapter 3).**
The bible says Alexander throws alone at odd hours when he can't sleep. Once in the Grey Year, Niall finds him in the training yard at three in the morning, throwing badly. Niall says nothing, corrects his wrist, and goes back to bed. Neither mentions it at breakfast.
- *Adds:* Grief shown without a word of grief, and the romance carried through practical care.
- *Cost:* A paragraph inside 3.4.

**B3. The pantry door (1.1, 5.5, 25.x).**
In 1.1 he passes the butler's pantry without looking at it. At 5.5 he learns why Will Pryce vanished. When he comes home as Duke, he stops at the pantry door, opens it, and looks at an ordinary room with a bench where a boy used to wait for him. Then he closes it.
- *Adds:* A small private arc that closes in Book One and still leaves Will for Book Three.
- *Cost:* A few lines in 25.1 or 25.4.

### Niall

**C1. The first of the month (Chapter 3, paid off at 10.4 and in Book Two).**
Through the Grey Year, Niall rides into Norwich on the first of every month and comes back with nothing bought. Alexander notices and never asks.
- *Adds:* A mystery about the man the reader is falling for. At 10.4 the reader connects the errand to "Four years."
- *Canon:* Niall's Habits field: "a few shillings a month to Calum's mother through the bookbinder." Book Two reveals it. Keep the destination off the page.
- *Cost:* Two or three lines across Chapter 3.

**C2. The lamp under his door (Chapters 3, 11, or 25).**
Alexander sees lamplight under Niall's door at two in the morning, more than once. It is the cipher in Alasdair's notebook, which Niall works at on sleepless nights. Alexander assumes it is something else, and does not knock.
- *Canon:* Niall's Habits field. The cipher breaks in Book Six.
- *Cost:* A line.

**C3. A sister he doesn't talk about. YOUR CALL.**
Niall supports his sister Isla in Portobello. Does Alexander know she exists? If not, a letter in a child's hand arriving for Mr. Fraser in the Grey Year would show the reader that Niall has a whole life he keeps out of the castle, and give Alexander something he cannot ask about.
- *Your call:* Whether Alexander knows of Isla before Book Two.

### Catherine

**D1. Her mother's letters in the atlas (15.4, paid off in Book Four).**
The bible says Amelia's letters to her foreign contacts "are the letters Catherine pasted into her atlas." At 15.4 the family finds the atlas open in Catherine's room. Amelia is in that scene. She turns a page and finds her own letters pasted in among the routes, postmarked from places she has never told her children she writes to. Mother learns that the daughter's longing was fed by her own secret life.
- *Adds:* A shattering private moment for Amelia, hours before her death. Alexander sees his mother's face and doesn't understand it, which seeds Book Four's discovery of her papers.
- *Canon:* Amelia's Habits field. Must not explain the letters' content in Book One.
- *Cost:* A paragraph in 15.4.

**D2. Calum's unspoken devotion (3.3, 10.5, 16.1, 22.1, 25.6–25.7).**
The bibles fix it: Calum loves her and never says so, and she knows and never names it, answering with fondness and teasing that never becomes a promise. Let it run as a thread through her interludes. At the close, the salt-stained atlas on her table at 25.7 has been rebound in the sanctuary by Calum, who mends damaged books (25.6). The stains are still there. Only the spine is new.
- *Adds:* A second, quieter love story that mirrors the first: also unspoken, also careful.
- *Canon:* Calum's and Catherine's Secrets and When in Love fields. It must never tip into romance on the page.
- *Cost:* A line in 25.6 and a detail in 25.7.

**D3. The mirror (7.3, 25.7).**
She turns away from mirrors because the face isn't hers yet. At 25.7 her mirror is turned to the wall (already decided). Let Alexander, not Catherine, be the one who turned it, before she came home, without being asked. She notices. It's the one piece of his protectiveness she accepts.
- *Adds:* Shows his protective instinct used well, once, inside the arc where he learns to stop overriding her.
- *Cost:* A line.

### Edward

**E1. Who judges the marrows (1.1, 25.4).**
When Alexander comes home, Thomas asks, carefully, who will judge the marrows at next year's festival. It is the first ordinary question anyone has asked the new Duke, and it nearly undoes him.
- *Adds:* Grief arriving through the smallest thing, and the 1.1 dispute paid off.
- *Cost:* Two lines in 25.4.

**E2. Merit or debt (11.4, 12.x).**
Edward and Niall carry the same unanswerable question: was Edward's care for Niall friendship, or a debt to Niall's dead father? At the camp, Edward thanks Niall for bringing Alexander, and neither man is sure what the thanks is for. Alexander sees it from across the fire.
- *Canon:* Both men's Insecurities fields. The Alasdair debt stays unresolved.
- *Cost:* A short exchange.

### Amelia

**F1. The children she lost. YOUR CALL.**
Amelia's bible lists "the lost pregnancies." At 11.5, in the argument over her letter, she could say it to Edward in front of Alexander: "I have lost children before, Edward. I will not lose this one to your caution." It gives her "Bring him" a reason older than the Vanguard, and gives her death a different weight.
- *Your call:* Whether Alexander learns of the lost pregnancies in Book One.

**F2. The unforgiven (13.3, 15.3).**
Amelia tells Seraphina she has not forgiven her (13.3). She never does, because she dies the next night. In the refuge at 15.3, where Seraphina reads the relief by touch with her good eye failing, Amelia needs her help with a working and asks for it plainly. She does not forgive her. She asks, and Seraphina gives it.
- *Adds:* Seraphina's lifelong want ("to be forgiven by Amelia, which death forecloses") gets its closest approach, and its denial.
- *Canon:* Seraphina's Dreams field. Both women are in 15.3. Seraphina is not in 17.3, where Amelia dies.
- *Cost:* A line.

### Calum

**G1. A book in his hands (10.3, 16.x, 25.6).**
Calum's habit is mending books wherever he finds them. In the refuge (Chapter 16), let him be holding a damaged book when the family arrives, Hessian's or the refuge's, and set it down carefully before he is commanded to fight. The reader sees a bookbinder before a weapon.
- *Cost:* A detail.

### Seraphina

**H1. One braid (Chapters 13, 20).**
Her amulet holds a braid of hair from each of her dead coven. She once loved someone in the Circle and does not speak of it. Let her fingers find one braid more often than the others. Nobody asks.
- *Adds:* A grief the reader can sense and never solve in Book One.
- *Cost:* A detail, twice.

### Beatrice

**I1. The protocol she bends (24.5).**
Her secret is that she bends procedure for people who prove themselves, because she was once given no room. Show it once without comment: after the second assault (24.5), Ellery reports that her operatives took orders from the Duke in the field, which no protocol allows, since Beatrice holds command. Beatrice says, "I didn't hear that." Then, a moment later: "You'll do."
- *Cost:* Two lines.

### Percival

**J1. The legend and the scar (7.5, 24.2).**
He retells his own legend of Loch Maree, and the truth of the scar is his secret. In 7.5, let him rehearse a version of the scar's story in the hotel mirror, adjusting it. At 24.2, watching the manor burn, he says "Sterling" with respect, and for once does not tell himself a story about it.
- *Adds:* An antagonist who lies to himself, which is more frightening than one who doesn't.
- *Cost:* Lines inside existing interludes.

### Hessian

**K1. The curious guest (1.4).**
The bible says that in company Hessian gives "a guest's performance, anthropological," curious about English customs. At dinner, let him ask earnest questions about the marrow judging: how an honest marrow is told from a watered one. The table laughs. Alexander doesn't.
- *Adds:* Charm with something underneath it. It echoes Edward's "whether it is honest" from 1.1.
- *Cost:* A short exchange.

## PART FOUR: SIDE PLOTS FOR THE SUPPORTING CAST

**L1. The Widow Carrow's son (2.5, 3.4, 25.1).**
Carrow, whom Edward sends riding north the night Catherine vanishes, is the Widow Carrow's son. The woman whose marrow was suspected in 1.1 spends the Grey Year waiting for news of a son out on the northern road for the Duke's daughter. At the homecoming, the sheaf from the Fen left at the gate under the crape is hers. This time Alexander counts all eleven households.
- *Adds:* One tenant family the reader follows from comedy to grief to loyalty. It pays off Alexander's count from 1.1.
- *Canon:* Supporting Bible: "Carrow is the Widow Carrow's son." Where Carrow is in the Grey Year is not fixed. Keep it to his absence.
- *Cost:* Lines in 3.4 and 25.1.

**L2. Why Thomas stays (1.1, 25.4).**
His father, John, works the hay cart on a ruined knee and has been kept on by the family when "most estates, a man goes lame, he goes without wage the same week." Thomas's loyalty has a reason, and he never says it to Alexander until 25.4, when he is offered the stewardship and says it as his acceptance.
- *Canon:* Thomas's Speech field. John and Mary appear in person in Book Two; here they are only mentioned.
- *Cost:* One line.

**L3. Bess keeps the jar (3.2, 25.x).**
Bess was the only one who knew the pin-money jar existed. Through the Grey Year she makes sure no one moves it, which is why it is still there at 25.7. When Catherine comes home, Bess asks to wait on her again, and Alexander has to refuse, because of the seclusion.
- *Adds:* A minor character the reader roots for, and a visible cost to "kept out of sight."
- *Canon:* The author's ruling on Catherine's seclusion. Bess must learn nothing of the truth.
- *Cost:* A short exchange in 25.1 or 25.4.

**L4. Dr. Hale, kept ignorant (Chapter 3, 25.x).**
The household physician is "kept permanently ignorant, the family's running joke," full of questions nobody answers. Give him one Grey Year scene of fussy, kindly questions that every Sterling deflects in a different way. In Chapter 25, he asks to examine Lady Catherine and is refused, and goes away hurt rather than suspicious. He is the only person in Norfolk who would have the skill to notice something wrong, and he is never allowed to.
- *Adds:* Comedy with a sharp edge, and a man the reader likes.
- *Canon:* He keeps Amelia's dressings, which become evidence in Book Four. Do not mention them.
- *Cost:* A half-scene, and a line.

**L5. Gideon's word (21.4).**
Gideon is stung by any suggestion that his word is bad. At 21.4, Sebastian calls him a smuggler whose word is worth what he is paid. Alexander answers with something he saw in Alwinton: "He paid a debt there he could have walked away from." Gideon says nothing. At 25.5 he names no rate.
- *Adds:* Shows how Gideon's loyalty is won.
- *Cost:* Two lines.

**L6. The boy in the cage (15.2, 25.5).**
The captive boy is about twelve, the same age as Davey Coop (7.1). Let Alexander see Gideon look at the boy and understand why Gideon chooses to stay. At 25.5, "a boy to see about" then means two boys at once.
- *Cost:* One line.

**L7. Mrs. Thompson's hands (25.1).**
Her tell is her hands folded hard in her apron when she has decided not to cry. When the staff line forms behind the new Duke, she is the one whose hands Alexander sees.
- *Cost:* A detail.

**L8. Ewan's cap (4.2, 18.5).**
Ewan turns his cap once in his hands when he is nervous. In his interlude at 18.5, alone with Edward's packet, he does it with no one there to see, and stops himself, because there is no one left to be nervous in front of.
- *Cost:* A detail.

## PART FIVE: MYSTERY AND PRESSURE

**M1. The coachman's wrists (1.3, 19.1).**
At 1.3 Alexander notices the driver of the crestless carriage: a colorless young man, cuffs pulled down over bandaged wrists, eyes that hold nothing of their own. At 19.1, when the man walks down the shingle with Hessian's terms, Alexander recognizes him. His canon question, whether anyone waits for Josef at home, then comes out of recognition.
- *Canon:* Josef drives the carriage, unnamed (Supporting Bible).

**M2. Higgins hesitates (8.2–8.4).**
In the inn fire, Higgins touches the knife scar at his shoulder as he looks at Niall, and calls his men off the burning stable. It should read as calculation that might be something else.
- *Canon:* He becomes a source who can be turned in Book Two.

**M3. Catherine at the door (Chapter 20).**
At dawn, as Catherine goes to her day's rest, she is found at the threshold of the hidden room, facing north, with the door unbarred from the inside. She does not remember doing it.
- *Adds:* The pull as a live danger in the talkiest stretch of the back half.
- *Canon:* The Dossier says a maker can stir his progeny's emotions, and the pull presses at the bite.

**M4. A face in the refuge (Chapter 15). YOUR CALL.**
In Hessian's rooms, a miniature of a woman kept for four centuries: Elisabeta. Alexander sees a family likeness he can't place.
- *Canon:* Hessian's Secrets field already fixes that Catherine resembles Elisabeta because she descends from her. The resemblance is canon.
- *Your call:* Whether to put it on the page in Book One. Doing so seeds Book Four's bloodline revelation early, and lets an attentive reader guess it.
