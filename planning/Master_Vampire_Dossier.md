## PART ONE: THE NATURE OF VAMPIRES

### What a Vampire Is

A vampire is a human being who has died of an ancient disease and been brought back by the blood of the vampire who infected them. Vampires are neither conventionally alive nor conventionally dead. The body has died and goes on living, its heart kept beating by the human blood it drinks. The public believes vampires are stories. The Shadow Vanguard knows better.

Vampires can live indefinitely and do not age. They need blood to live and human blood to thrive. They are stronger, faster, and keener of sense than any human, and they are vulnerable to sunlight, silver, fire, and the destruction of the heart or head.

### The Fundamental Principle

Vampirism is a condition, not a moral identity. Becoming a vampire does not make anyone evil. A vampire remains capable of love, loyalty, compassion, cruelty, sacrifice, hatred, remorse, and moral choice.

Vampirism does bring powerful pressures that can wear a person down over time: blood hunger, great physical power, an unending life, isolation from humanity, the repeated loss of mortal loved ones, predatory instinct, the influence of a maker, the pull of vampire culture, and centuries in which ordinary human life grows ever more distant. Many ancient vampires lose their emotional connection with humanity under that weight. Some come to regard humans as food, servants, possessions, entertainment, or brief creatures whose individual lives hardly matter.

This decline is common, but it is not inevitable. A vampire who keeps up relationships, principles, empathy, purpose, and ties to the living can resist it. Calum is evidence that the Turning does not destroy conscience. Hessian shows what happens when grief, entitlement, and power are allowed to replace it.

### Origin

Vampirism is an ancient disease, and its origin is unknown. No surviving vampire, Vanguard archive, occult text, or historical record gives a definitive account. Theories include a natural disease of great age, a disease corrupted by magic, an ancient curse, deliberate experiment by a lost civilization, or the intervention of some unknown power. The series never settles the question; the disease's origin stays as uncertain on the page as it is to the people living with it.

Accounts that may describe vampires reach back into deep antiquity, some as far as the first Sumerian cities. Those oldest traditions are fragmentary and hard to separate from myths of demons, spirits, revenants, and other blood-drinkers. The oldest reliable traditions gather in the north and east of Europe, though the disease may be far older than they are.

### Vlad Dracula

Vlad Dracula is tied to vampire history, but the truth is deliberately uncertain. Within supernatural circles, several possibilities are discussed. Vlad was a vampire. He encountered vampires. Vampire activity attached itself to his reputation, or a vampire used that reputation. He belonged to a vampire bloodline. Later folklore merged several historical figures into one legend.

The truth is this. Vlad was a man. Hessian's own sire, Dragomir, served at Vlad's court, and many of the darkest stories later told of Vlad were Dragomir's deeds. Hessian, a Transylvanian of the same century, knows this and has never said so. The truth stays hidden on the page until Book Six, when Dragomir enters the story, and it shows that the popular Dracula stories hold both genuine history and a great deal of embellishment.

## PART TWO: INFECTION AND THE TURNING

### The Disease

The disease lives in a vampire's saliva. It passes into a human bloodstream through a bite, or through saliva entering an open wound. A vampire's fangs deliver saliva directly into torn tissue and blood, which makes the bite the surest means of infection.

A vampire's blood does not carry the disease. The blood carries something else entirely (see "The Saving Drink," below).

### Untreated Infection

An infected human sickens quickly and dies within a day or two. A vampire who drinks enough can kill sooner through blood loss.

The course of the illness brings fever and chills, pallor, weakness, a disordered heartbeat, raging thirst, confusion, and disturbed senses, ending in collapse and death. How fast it runs depends on the amount of saliva introduced, where the wound lies, and the health of the victim. No ordinary medicine can stop it.

### The Saving Drink

There is only one way to survive the disease: the infected person must drink the blood of the vampire who bit them. That vampire's blood carries a property that works with the disease rather than against it. The disease still completes its work and the body still dies, but the maker's blood carries the person through death and brings them back as a vampire. This is the Turning.

Only the biter's blood will serve. Blood from any other vampire cannot save the infected. If the vampire who bit someone is dead, far away, or unwilling, the bitten person dies.

The saving drink is given once. After it, the new vampire lives on human blood and never needs its maker's blood again.

There is no partial state. A person is either human, dying of the disease, or a vampire. The only thing that separates the dying from the turned is the drink.

### Consent

The bite can be forced on anyone. The saving drink is something the bitten person does, and so the Turning can hinge on the victim's will. A maker who wants a willing progeny, or who needs one for a magical purpose, must win that will before the bite, because after it there are only a day or two left.

This is why Hessian spent a year compelling Catherine toward a yes before he bit her. A drink taken under compulsion completes the Turning all the same. Whether it counts as true consent is a question of magic and morals, not biology. The northern seal, which answers only to a willing Catherine, recognizes her compelled yes only in part.

### The Death and Return

The Turning is a true death. The heart falters and stops, breathing ceases, and the body cools. Then, carried by the maker's blood, the heart takes up an altered rhythm and the person wakes as a vampire, pale and cold, with sunken eyes.

The first hours are hard. The first hunger is often severe. The senses arrive all at once and overwhelm: sounds too loud, scents too sharp, every heartbeat in the room impossible to ignore. Strength and speed are unfamiliar, and movement comes faster than the new vampire intends.

### Learning Control

In the weeks and months after the return, a new vampire must learn to control feeding, strength, and speed. They must learn to filter the flood of the senses, to keep out of the sun, and to live among humans. Above all, they must learn to tell hunger apart from intention.

A responsible maker guides a new vampire through this period. A vampire made and abandoned, or made as a weapon, learns it alone or not at all.

### Feeding Without Infecting

Because the disease lives in saliva, blood taken without a bite carries no infection. A vampire who drinks from a cup filled through a controlled cut can feed without killing or turning anyone. This is the practice of conscientious vampires and of every vampire under Vanguard protection. It is also how Catherine is fed.

## PART THREE: BLOOD

### Blood Is Life

To a vampire, blood is not simply food. It keeps the heart beating, and so it keeps everything else going: the body, the appearance of health, strength and speed, healing, and a steady mind. Severe injuries sharply increase a vampire's need for blood.

### Human Blood

Human blood is what a vampire truly requires. It must drink at least once every few days. Without human blood a vampire cannot heal properly, and over time it cannot stay itself.

The blood does not have to come from a killed victim. A willing donor who gives through a controlled cut feeds a vampire as fully as any hunt.

### Blood Spoils

Blood clots and sours within hours of leaving the body, and nothing known in the period prevents it. Vampires must therefore feed fresh or close to it. A vampire under protection needs living donors near at hand, not a store in a cellar.

Any preparation that could keep blood usable for days would be priceless to vampires, to the Vanguard, and to the Institute alike.

### Animal Blood and the Wasting

Animal blood keeps a vampire alive for a time, but it is not enough. A vampire living only on animal blood begins to waste. Over a span of a few weeks to a couple of months it mutates into a **Gaunt**: a thin, dark-grey creature of human shape, with longer fangs, a thirst that never stops, and no mind left except the urge to kill.

Vanguard physicians call the process "the wasting." It is irreversible once complete. When a vampire has gone Gaunt, human blood can no longer bring it back.

A Gaunt is the one kind of vampire the Vanguard destroys without judging its choices, because it no longer has any to make.

### Mixed Blood

Human blood diluted with animal blood holds off the wasting but leaves a vampire weak. Its strength, speed, and healing all fall short of what they would be. This is the diet Hessian keeps Calum on. It keeps him from wasting and keeps him weak enough to control.

### Hunger and the Heartbeat

A vampire's hunger passes through recognizable states. A **fed** vampire has full control and full strength. A **hungry** vampire finds blood increasingly hard to ignore and grows irritable and distracted. A **starved** vampire weakens as its predatory instincts sharpen. Past that lies **frenzy**, in which extreme hunger overwhelms deliberate control.

Frenzy can also be triggered by catastrophic injury or by a sudden flood of fresh blood. It does not erase the person. Strong bonds of feeling can still reach a vampire in frenzy. A vampire who lets itself starve bears responsibility for what follows. Acts committed in a frenzy the vampire did not choose call for harder moral judgment.

A vampire's heartbeat tracks its hunger. A well-fed vampire's heart beats steady and strong. As its last feeding recedes, the beat slows and weakens, so a trained ear can tell how long ago a vampire last drank.

Frenzy and the wasting are different things. Frenzy is a passing loss of control. The wasting is a permanent change of the body.

### Starvation and Mummification

A vampire that drinks nothing at all does not die. It grows thin, dry, and hollow-eyed, then its body dries out entirely and mummifies. A mummified vampire looks like a long-dead corpse, and it is not dead.

Human blood can revive it, even after years. Revival is gradual. The body fills out, the heart returns, and the vampire's strength and powers come back by degrees as it is fed. A mummified maker's bond with its progeny goes quiet and returns as the maker revives.

This is Hessian's state at the end of Book One.

### Supernatural Blood

The blood of supernatural beings can differ greatly from human blood. Depending on the species, it may nourish, do nothing, intoxicate, poison, carry active magic, or addict. Vampires learn not to feed carelessly from supernatural creatures.

### Vampire Blood and Other Vampires

Apart from the single saving drink that completes a Turning, vampire blood gives vampires no nourishment. A vampire who feeds on another vampire falls ill. A larger amount poisons it, and enough can kill it. This is the paradox of vampire blood: it is the one thing that can save a dying human, and it is poison to a vampire already made.

### Blood Memory

Blood sometimes carries traces of the person it came from: an emotion, a sensation, an image, a fragment of memory. The effect is unreliable. A vampire cannot drink someone's blood and learn everything they know. Strong memories, trauma, and magical events leave the likeliest impressions.

### Vampire Blood and Humans

A human who drinks vampire blood is not infected, because the disease lives in saliva, not blood. The blood is powerfully addictive to humans. It brings euphoria, mild hallucinations that are pleasant rather than frightening, and heightened strength and senses.

Repeated use breeds dependency, until the body expects the blood. Withdrawal brings fever and shaking, vomiting and pain, anxiety and desperate craving, weakness, and in the worst cases aggression and violence. Dependency gives vampires a hold over human servants and feeds an illicit trade in vampire blood.

### Familiars

A **familiar** is a human who keeps up a blood relationship with a vampire without being turned. The arrangement may be freely chosen or forced. A familiar enjoys the pleasures and heightened strength of the blood. In exchange, the familiar lives with dependency and withdrawal, with vulnerability to the vampire's influence, with deep emotional attachment, and with a vampire who can find and manipulate them.

Josef, Hessian's familiar since 1841, shows how far the bond can hollow a person. Finlay MacKenzie, who enters in Book Five, shows its darkest form among ordinary people.

## PART FOUR: THE BODY

### Aging

Full vampires stop aging at their death and return. A well-fed vampire looks healthy and about the age at which it was turned. That youth is kept up by blood. Hunger thins and hollows a vampire and makes it look older, and human blood restores it.

Catherine will not grow older.

### Child Vampires

A child who is turned never grows up in body, but the mind keeps developing. A vampire who looks twelve may carry decades or centuries of memory, learning, and grief. Turning a child is one of the most abhorrent acts in vampire culture, and most moderate vampire societies forbid it.

### Reproduction

Vampires are believed incapable of bearing or fathering children. The Turning is their only way of making new vampires and continuing a bloodline. Some ancient accounts claim otherwise, but none is reliable.

Hessian's child with Elisabeta was born before his Turning, while he was still a man. It was an ordinary human birth, and the line that descends from it is human.

### The Heart

A vampire's heart beats, driven by the human blood it drinks. At rest it beats more slowly than a human heart. It quickens with feeding, combat, healing, strong emotion, and effort, and it weakens as hunger grows.

The heart is the center of a vampire's altered life, which is why it is the key to incapacitating or killing one.

### Breathing, Temperature, and Sleep

Vampires breathe, for speech, scent, habit, and the look of life, but they can go without air far longer than any human. Very old vampires can become unnaturally still and hardly seem to breathe.

Vampires are noticeably cold, the turned body cooler than a living one. Fresh feeding warms them for a time.

Vampires sleep less than humans, and old vampires less than young ones. A badly injured vampire may fall into a long, deep, healing sleep.

Vampires dream. Maker bonds and some blood magic can reach into those dreams, though this is never simple mind-reading.

### Disease and Pain

Vampires are immune to most ordinary human diseases, though they can carry them in their blood for a time. Supernatural diseases remain a danger.

Vampires feel pain, but far less than humans do. Wounds that would fell a man are painful but bearable. Sunlight, silver, fire, and magical injury hurt far more.

### Healing, Regeneration, and Scars

Vampires heal quickly, but only while they drink human blood every few days. A vampire starved of human blood heals slowly or not at all.

Minor wounds close within minutes. Major wounds take hours or days. Organs can regrow. A severed limb can be reattached if recovered quickly, and regrowing a lost one takes a very long time and a great deal of blood. Healing burns through a vampire's blood, so a wounded vampire that is also hungry is at its weakest.

Most wounds heal without scars. Scars from before the Turning remain. Silver, fire, and powerful magic can leave lasting marks. A maker's bite can leave one too: the bite beneath Catherine's collarbone has never closed. It stays faintly cold and dark, and it is where Hessian's pull presses.

### Food, Drink, and Poison

A vampire can eat small amounts of ordinary food. It gives little or no nourishment, and too much brings nausea. Some vampires keep eating a little for the taste, the ritual, or the company.

Alcohol affects them only weakly. Alcohol or drugs already carried in a victim's blood reach them much more strongly. Ordinary poisons have weak or passing effects. Supernatural toxins, magical poisons, silver compounds, and certain venoms are truly dangerous.

### Appearance and Mirrors

The turned are pale and cold, with sunken eyes. Vampires cast reflections like anything else. They avoid mirrors because they can hardly bear how they now look.

Specially prepared magical mirrors can reveal concealment magic, blood magic, or corruption that ordinary sight misses, which may be one source of the mirror legends.

### Cosmetics

To pass among the living, vampires use the cosmetics of the day. Pearl powder covers the pallor, rouge counterfeits warm blood, and belladonna drops hide the red that rises in the eyes with hunger. Making a vampire look normal is a long process, so one caught unprepared by a visitor is easy to spot. By candlelight and at night, a vampire with time to prepare passes unnoticed.

### Eyes and Fangs

Vampires see very well in low light, though not in total darkness. With hunger, feeding, anger, or effort, their eyes may widen, darken, catch light strangely, or take on a faint red tint. They do not glow.

Vampire fangs are subtle at rest and lengthen with hunger, feeding, and aggression. A Gaunt's fangs are larger than any ordinary vampire's.

## PART FIVE: POWERS

### Strength, Speed, and Reflexes

Even a newly turned vampire is several times stronger than an ordinary human. Strength grows with age, bloodline, feeding, and training, and ancient vampires can be extraordinarily powerful.

Vampire speed comes in explosive bursts rather than constant impossible motion. Over short distances in a fight, a vampire moves faster than the human eye comfortably follows. Its reflexes let it answer attacks that would overwhelm a man. Guns and blades still threaten it, but landing a blow usually takes surprise, numbers, confinement, distraction, or Vanguard tactics.

### Hearing Blood

A vampire hears blood pumping in any living creature, human or animal, within a certain distance. No fixed range applies. It depends on how loud the heart is, walls and other barriers, wind and weather, and the vampire's age, hunger, and experience. Exposed blood is noticed from farther away still, by scent as much as sound.

### The Halo and the Haze

A vampire sees a halo of light around humans who are healthy and fit, their blood making them glow. Around the unwell, the diseased, and the sick it sees a dark haze instead, almost like fog.

### Reading People

Heartbeat, breath, scent, temperature, blood flow, and the small movements of muscle let a vampire recognize fear, attraction, anger, illness, stress, and lies. To humans this can look like mind-reading, but it is only perception.

Together with the halo and the haze, it lets a vampire read the health of a whole crowded room at a glance. That makes vampires valuable, and dangerous, to anyone hunting the sick or the strong. It also gives Catherine an unexpected use in the Vanguard's work.

### Recognizing Other Vampires

Vampires can usually recognize one another at close range by scent, heartbeat, temperature, movement, and a sense of shared blood. Experienced vampires can sometimes tell members of a related bloodline. Concealment magic interferes with all of this.

### Compulsion

Many vampires can influence human minds, some slightly and some powerfully. Ancient vampires are usually the strongest.

Compulsion works best on what is already there. It can magnify a desire, create urgency, suppress resistance, encourage rationalization, redirect attention, press toward obedience, blur memory, and breed dependency. At its strongest it can build an infatuation that feels like love from within, as Hessian built in Catherine a longing to be his bride. It cannot make that love true, and it cannot make consent whole. What it builds is a counterfeit, resting on the victim's own longings turned toward the one who compels.

Compulsion weakens as the victim learns to tell their own desires from the compeller's use of them. It is also weakened by a strong sense of self, by training and prepared mental anchors, by magical protection, and by emotional ties to others. Contradiction and distance weaken it further, as does simple awareness of the manipulation. Vanguard operatives can be trained to recognize and resist it.

### What Vampires Cannot Do

Vampires cannot read minds. Maker bonds, blood magic, dreams, compulsion, and blood memory can pass impressions that resemble telepathy, but no vampire can simply hear another's thoughts.

Vampires cannot change shape. Bats, wolves, and mist are not vampire powers. Calum's wolf and raptor forms come from elsewhere. He is also a werewolf, a secret revealed in Book Three.

### Bloodlines and Gifts

Every vampire belongs to a lineage of makers and progeny. A bloodline can shape strength, magical aptitude, compulsion, healing, perception, and rarer abilities, and older bloodlines tend to be more potent.

A few vampires develop special gifts through bloodline, age, temperament, training, or study. These can include influence over dreams, exceptional compulsion, blood divination, concealment, influence over memory, influence over animals, or unusual perception. Such gifts are exceptions, never the rule.

### Concealment

Hessian can use ancient magic to hide what he is from supernatural perception. Observers overlook what is wrong, explain away the impossible, or feel vaguely uneasy without knowing why. An experienced eye may still catch the signs:

- unnatural stillness;
- a drop in temperature;
- strange movement of the air;
- animals behaving oddly;
- a distortion that magic-users can feel;
- small inconsistencies in what the senses report.

Other vampires can learn concealment, but Hessian is exceptionally skilled.

## PART SIX: WEAKNESSES AND DEATH

### Sunlight

True sunlight burns vampires, because their skin is dead. Artificial light does not: candlelight, gaslight, and limelight are harmless, and moonlight is only reflected sun and does no harm either. Overcast days are safer than clear ones, which is why vampires come out only in darkness or under cloud.

Brief exposure will not kill, but the skin blackens quickly enough to be seen. After about thirty minutes in the sun, the skin is burned beyond repair. The muscles beneath begin to burn, blood can no longer circulate through the burned flesh, and without circulation the vampire dies.

### Silver

Silver reacts with vampire blood and destroys its corpuscles. A cut from a silver blade wounds a vampire and heals poorly. Silver lodged inside the body kills unless it is removed quickly. Silver also disrupts some vampire magic, and driven into Calum's Blood-Mark it can break Hessian's hold on him (see Part Seven).

### Fire

Fire is deadly. A vampire can heal burns, but once fire reaches the heart and the heart burns, the vampire dies.

### The Heart and the Stake

A stake through the heart, or any serious wound to it, incapacitates a vampire and stops it fighting. It does not kill. If the stake is drawn and the vampire fed, it recovers.

Removing the heart, or severing it, kills.

### Decapitation

Severing the head kills a vampire.

### Iron and Holy Things

Iron is not poisonous to vampires, but cold iron can disrupt some magic, rituals, and protections.

Vampires are not demonic, and no symbol harms them by its shape. A cross is only wood or metal. True consecration and genuine ritual can still disrupt compulsion, blood magic, and concealment, and can raise protective boundaries. A vampire may be religious. Vampirism neither proves nor disproves any faith.

### What Does Not Work

Garlic has no power over vampires. Its smell can irritate their heightened senses, and folklore has turned that irritation into a weakness.

Vampires cross running water freely. Natural waterways can disrupt some tracking spells and blood magic, which may be where the legend began.

Vampires do not need an invitation to enter an ordinary home. Magically protected houses, ancient estates, sanctuaries, and blood-bound hereditary holdings do respond to ownership, inheritance, guardianship, invitation, and lawful standing. That is the deeper truth behind the invitation legend. It is why Hessian needed to buy the northern tract lawfully, and why the Sterling covenant answers to rightful authority.

Starvation does not kill. It mummifies (see Part Three).

### Photography

Vampires can be photographed. Powerful concealment magic may blur or distort an image, leaving shadows, strange exposures, or missing detail. The wet collodion process, new in 1851, produces sharper and more easily copied images than ever before. That makes it a danger to any vampire who must not be recorded.

### How to Kill a Vampire

A vampire is permanently killed by removing or destroying its heart, by severing its head, by burning it until the heart burns, by long exposure to true sunlight, or by silver left inside its body. For certainty, more than one method is used.

A mummified vampire is not a dead one. Neither is a vampire merely staked. Neither should ever be treated as destroyed.

### Vanguard Destruction Protocol

When a vampire must be destroyed, Vanguard procedure is as follows:

1. Immobilize it, with a stake to the heart if necessary.
2. Keep it from any source of blood.
3. Use silver to weaken it.
4. Remove or destroy the heart.
5. Sever the head.
6. Burn the remains when the vampire is old or dangerous, taking care that the heart is consumed.
7. Secure any blood, teeth, and remains, both from those who would use them and from anyone who might try a revival.

A mummified body found anywhere is to be treated as a sleeping vampire, never as a corpse.

## PART SEVEN: MAKERS AND PROGENY

### The Maker Bond

The vampire whose bite and blood turned another is its **maker**, and the one turned is its **progeny**. Older traditions use other words: sire, blood-parent, master, progenitor.

The Turning forges a bond between them. Through it a maker can more easily sense its progeny, compel them, stir their emotions, reach into their dreams, know their condition, and sharpen or dull their hunger. The bond is strongest soon after the Turning. It weakens with time and with distance, and it weakens further as the progeny resists.

A maker's death does not kill its progeny, though it strikes them with disorientation, pain, grief, weakness, nightmares, or a sudden emptiness. A mummified maker's bond falls silent and returns as the maker is revived.

### Control Is Not Ownership

An ordinary maker bond never gives full possession of another vampire's body or mind. Progeny can resist, and a strong personality, age, distance, training, and magical protection all strengthen that resistance.

### Catherine's Bite

Catherine's bite is the seat of Hessian's bond with her. The mark beneath her collarbone never closes. It stays faintly cold and dark, and his pull presses there. After his defeat the pull falls silent. As he is slowly revived in the cairn, it slowly regains its strength, so her own body measures his return.

### The Blood-Mark

Hessian's hold on Calum goes far beyond an ordinary bond. Calum carries a **Blood-Mark** over his heart. It is an ancient blood-magic mechanism that ties Hessian's control directly to Calum's blood and heart. During an active command, Hessian can seize Calum's will entirely.

Silver driven or pressed into the Mark during that control can break the connection. Because silver destroys a vampire's blood and the Mark sits over the heart, doing so risks stopping Calum's heart, crippling him, or killing him.

The Blood-Mark is enslavement. It is not part of any natural Turning, and it must be destroyed separately from anything done to the maker. At the climax of *Inheritance*, silver breaks Calum's Mark for good. It cannot be restored, and only its scar remains.

### Thralls

A **thrall** is any person or being whose will has been badly compromised by compulsion, blood magic, or conditioning. The word describes a state of control, not a species. A human, a familiar, a progeny, or another vampire can each be a thrall.

Calum is Hessian's progeny, and through the Blood-Mark an unwilling thrall.

### Do Makers Own Their Progeny?

Ancient traditions treat progeny as the property of their makers. Reformist traditions reject that entirely. The question at the heart of vampire politics is whether making someone's vampire body gives the maker any right over the person inside it.

The series' answer is no. Hessian's answer is yes, and that belief is the root of his cruelty to both Calum and Catherine.

## PART EIGHT: VAMPIRE SOCIETY

### Organization

There is no single vampire government. Vampires organize through bloodlines, Houses, courts, territories, alliances, councils, families, patronage, and personal loyalties centuries old. Traditions differ sharply from region to region.

**Age and status.** Age brings prestige. Older vampires usually command more strength, more wealth, wider connections, deeper magical knowledge, and centuries of gathered information. Age does not grant lawful authority, but it always commands attention.

**Houses.** Powerful bloodlines may form Houses. A House gathers an ancient progenitor, their progeny and later generations, human familiars and servants, allies, humans under its protection, and associated supernatural beings. Some Houses behave like aristocratic dynasties.

**Territory.** Ancient vampires may claim territory. Territorial agreements can govern who may live there, who may feed there, sanctuary, the making of progeny, and entry by outsiders. Some territories are magically bound to their rulers.

### Law and Taboo

Organized vampire communities may forbid killing without cause, feeding on protected humans, hunting in another House's territory, and careless infection. They may also require that anyone who makes a progeny accept responsibility for them. Predatory factions reject such rules.

Most moderate communities treat forced Turning as a grave violation. Other factions regard it as conquest, punishment, recruitment, or a maker's privilege. Turning a child is taboo almost everywhere, and even some predators find it abhorrent.

Vampires can swear **blood oaths**, promises bound by magic through blood. Breaking one may bring pain, weakness, loss of power, or whatever penalty the oath named. Ordinary promises carry no such force.

### Via Noctis

Via Noctis is a vampire political faction built on supernatural sovereignty, the belief that humans have no rightful authority over vampires or other supernatural beings. It does not speak for all vampires, and its members do not all agree. Some want isolation, some want peaceful self-government, and some believe humans are inferior. The most extreme among them want predation, domination, conquest, human servitude, and vampire supremacy.

Hessian stands toward the authoritarian and supremacist end without leading the faction. The series can therefore reveal vampires older than him, more powerful, opposed to him, frightened of him, allied with him, or disgusted by him, and Via Noctis can outlast his defeat as a continuing force.

### Living Among Humans

Vampires keep away from public life and move often to places where no one knows them. They travel in dark carriages without windows and come out only after dark or under heavy cloud. Before being seen, they spend long preparation on their faces.

Long-lived vampires must reinvent themselves again and again. They use false heirs and invented descendants, forged records, legal proxies, controlled estates, human agents, marriages of convenience, and transfers of property from one identity to the next. Ancient vampires can gather great wealth over centuries of ownership, and Hessian has had four hundred years to perfect these methods.

### Secrecy and Folklore

Vampires stay hidden through their own caution and self-policing, through Vanguard intervention and concealment magic, and through compulsion and destroyed evidence. False explanations and institutional secrecy help, and so does humanity's habit of explaining away the impossible. Many legends began as real encounters whose details warped in the telling.

Folklore holds real knowledge mixed with error.

**True.** Vampires drink blood and are stronger and faster than humans. They influence minds and live extraordinarily long. Sunlight harms them, and they make other vampires through the bite.

**Partly true.** The stake incapacitates but does not kill. Invitation matters only to bound and protected places. Running water troubles some magic but not the vampire. Mirrors trouble vampires for reasons of grief, not reflection.

**False.** Garlic does nothing. Vampires cast reflections and are not destroyed by crosses. Not every bite makes a vampire, since without the saving drink the bitten simply die.

## PART NINE: THE VANGUARD, THE INSTITUTE, AND VAMPIRES

### How the Vanguard Judges Vampires

The Shadow Vanguard does not treat all vampires as enemies. It judges them by what they do, asking:

- whether a vampire is attacking people;
- whether its feeding is consented to;
- whether it is infecting humans;
- whether anyone has been turned by force;
- whether the vampire is itself being controlled;
- whether it is violating sanctuary;
- whether it needs protection, perhaps from another faction or from the Institute.

A peaceful vampire can receive the Vanguard's protection. A Gaunt cannot, because nothing is left in it to protect or to reason with.

### Sanctuary and Blood Supply

A vampire granted sanctuary may receive protection, blood, medical and magical help, secure shelter by day, and help mastering its hunger. Sanctuary does not exempt it from the laws that protect others.

Because blood spoils within hours, the Vanguard cannot keep a store of it. A protected vampire must be fed fresh from willing donors by cut and cup. That makes every protected vampire a standing arrangement of trust between the vampire, its donors, and the few who know. In a lean or desperate season, animal blood can bridge a short gap, never a long one.

### Detection

No single sign proves a vampire. Taken together, the signs may include cool skin, a slow heartbeat, heavy cosmetics, unnatural stillness, shallow breathing, strange eyes, and wounds that heal too fast. Others are an unusual reaction to blood or to silver, a faint absence of human scent, and a life led wholly by night or behind closed carriage windows.

Animals often sense that something is wrong. Dogs grow defensive, horses nervous, and prey animals freeze or bolt. Hessian's unnaturally quiet horses show how an experienced vampire can master that reaction.

Vampires smell subtly unlike humans. The young and the recently fed smell more human, and the old and the starving less.

### Combat Doctrine

A vampire is never to be fought as though it were merely a strong man. Vanguard doctrine calls for working as a team and keeping distance. Operatives carry silver, guard the throat and arteries, and keep blood out of sight. They control the ground, use the sun where they can, and contain rather than chase. They bring magical support where it exists, and they confirm the kill. Against an ancient vampire, operatives must also investigate its magical defenses before assuming it can be killed.

### Vampire Medicine

Vampire physiology is only partly understood. Research concerns the Turning, hunger and the wasting, maker bonds, healing, sunlight and silver injury, blood addiction in humans, and the possibility of a cure.

No reliable cure for vampirism is known at the start of the series. Harcourt distinguishes four degrees of hope: suppression, which eases symptoms; stabilization, which holds the condition where it is; reversal, which undoes part of the change; and a true cure, which restores full human life. Before Catherine, a true cure was thought all but impossible, since vampirism is not a sickness of the living but a death survived.

### Harcourt and the Ethics of Care

Dr. Miriam Harcourt begins with a clinician's instinct to study: the infection, the hunger, confinement, feeding, and treatment. Catherine forces a crucial distinction on her. A supernatural patient remains a person. Research, restraint, feeding arrangements, and treatment all require the patient's informed participation wherever it can be given. That lesson changes the Vanguard's medical practice for good.

### The Institute's Interest

The Ashcroft Institute has every reason to want vampires. Their bodies promise healing, long life, immunity to disease, strength, compulsion, and resistance to death. It wants their blood too, for the drug trade its addictiveness would support, and it wants their remains: blood, teeth, bone, organs, and tissue that keep supernatural properties for a time after death. It does not regard captured vampires as persons, which makes even a dangerous vampire a potential victim of Ashcroft exploitation.

Because only true sunlight burns vampires, the Institute's pursuit of an artificial sun through experiments with arc lamps is a natural line of weapons research. Once the Sterling bloodline and its effect on the disease are known, Catherine's and Alexander's blood become the most valuable specimens in Britain.

## PART TEN: KNOWN INDIVIDUALS

### Count Wilhelm Viktor Hessian

Hessian was born in Transylvania around 1450 and is about four hundred years old in 1852. As a man he was a castellan holding a border fortress, and a scholar. His courtesy was learned by observation rather than felt. He loved a human woman, Elisabeta, and before his Turning she bore him a child who survived without his knowledge. He was Turned in 1488, at thirty-eight. When Elisabeta saw what he had become, she sent their daughter, Ilona, to her sister in Hermannstadt and told him the child had died of fever. Within the year his sire killed Elisabeta to cut the last human tie that might call him back, and her death turned him toward cruelty. Catherine uncovers the truth in Book Six.

He escaped his sire, Dragomir, rather than destroying him. Dragomir taught him nothing, still exists, and killed Elisabeta. Hessian blames him and is right to. Everything Hessian knows of vampirism he learned alone.

Over centuries his grief curdled into obsession, his love into possession, and his memory into idealization, until he no longer told resemblance from identity, desire from consent, possession from devotion, or obedience from love. He believes that power grants authority, that love grants possession, and that immortality grants superiority. He believes a maker has rights over the turned, that human lives are brief and therefore lesser, and that Catherine's likeness to Elisabeta gives him a claim to her. His vampirism gives him the power to act on those beliefs. It did not create them. He is not evil because he is a vampire. He is evil because of what he chooses to believe.

He has an ancient vampire's strength, speed, endurance, senses, healing, awareness of blood, and compulsion, all highly developed. Beyond those, he has spent centuries of study acquiring sophisticated concealment magic, advanced blood magic, techniques of maker control including the Blood-Mark, ritual knowledge, and territorial magic. These are the fruits of study and preparation, not powers every vampire comes to in time.

He sits toward the supremacist end of Via Noctis without leading it. He believes his ancestors built the northern refuge; they did not.

At the end of Book One, burned and starved of blood, he drags himself into an ancient cairn in the northern heather and dries to a mummified husk. Josef waits there to guard him and, in time, to feed him back toward life. Josef is human, Hessian's familiar and thrall for eleven years, and he has only his own blood to give. He feeds the husk a little at a time while fighting his own withdrawal, so the revival is slow. As it proceeds, Hessian's pull on Catherine returns. It stirs late in Book Three, and Hessian walks again in Book Five.

### Catherine Rose Sterling

Catherine is a full vampire, turned by Hessian about two months before her rescue in the winter of 1852. For more than a year he used compulsion to confuse her, to make her believe she loved him, and to make her long to be his bride, so that she would take the saving drink willingly. Her yes was real, but it answered her own longing to leave, to travel, and to live an adventure, not him. Her rescue does not end his work in her. She must be brought back from the counterfeit love he built, and that recovery is part of her story.

Everything in this dossier applies to her:

- She must drink human blood every few days, fresh, from willing donors by cut and cup, or she will begin to waste.
- She cannot stay long in the sun, travels in closed carriages, and dresses her pallor with cosmetics.
- She wears gloves indoors so no one feels how cold her hands are.
- She avoids mirrors because the grey face and red-tinged eyes do not feel like hers.
- She counts other people's pulses involuntarily, hates that she can, and knows the habit means her hunger is rising.
- She will not age.

Her heightened senses let her read the health of a crowded room, a gift that gives her an unexpected place in the Vanguard's work.

Her blood holds something no physician has seen. While Alexander and Niall are in the north, Dr. Harcourt studies it from a brush carrying Catherine's blood, which she was examining when Alexander first met her. She finds a property she cannot identify, one that suggests the disease might one day be driven back and Catherine made human again. It will not work alone. It needs a further agent, some preparation or remedy, to help Catherine's blood fight the disease off. Finding that agent is the series-long search for a cure.

The property comes from Catherine's descent from Hessian and Elisabeta, a descent Alexander shares, and his blood carries the same property. The line's blood recognizes Hessian's strain of the disease as kin, and with the further agent it can refuse the disease and drive it out. The cure therefore reaches only those Turned within Hessian's line, Calum among them. The further agent is water from the holy well on Isle Maree, prepared with rowan by a method the kelpies of the loch preserved, recorded in cipher in Alasdair Fraser's notebook and recovered in Book Six. No one knows of the descent until Book Four.

Her story is not only whether she can become human again. It is who she chooses to be, whatever she becomes.

### Calum Hamish MacDuff

Calum is a full vampire, Turned against his will by Hessian himself in the winter of 1845, at nineteen, in Edinburgh. He keeps his conscience, his loyalty, his capacity for love, his guilt, his moral reasoning, and his ability to choose restraint.

He has a vampire's strength, speed, reflexes, senses, healing, and long life, and he is unusually skilled at detecting blood. Hessian keeps him on human blood mixed with animal blood, which holds off the wasting but leaves him weaker than a vampire of his age should be. He suffers blood hunger, sunlight, silver, Hessian's maker bond, the Blood-Mark over his heart, and a great weight of guilt.

His wolf and raptor forms are not vampire powers. He is also a werewolf, a secret revealed in Book Three. He prefers the wolf because it cannot speak, and so cannot apologize. He hates the raptor because Hessian made him scout in it.

Calum is living proof that becoming a vampire does not decide whether someone becomes a monster.

### Calum and Catherine

Calum can matter to Catherine as no human researcher can, because he knows from the inside what she is living. He knows what hunger feels like. He knows the flood of the senses, the fear of losing control, and the shame. He knows the maker's pull, and the difference between instinct and choice. He should guide her when she asks for his experience. He must never become one more person deciding what happens to her.

### Individuals in Later Books

**Elara.** Enters in Book Three. A vampire turned as a child who remains, in body, about ten to twelve years old while her mind has kept growing. Her story can explore stolen adulthood, immortality forced without consent, manipulation learned in order to survive, and decades or centuries lived in a child's body.

**Lucian Voss.** Enters in Book Three. A senior thrall in Hessian's orbit, kept apart from Josef: where Josef is hollowed out, Voss is ambitious. Where Calum fights to keep his will, Lucian decided that surrender was easier than resistance, and so he is Calum's dark mirror.

**Finlay MacKenzie.** Enters in Book Five. A human familiar whose long dependency on vampire blood shows the full cost of the bond, and the human face of the illicit trade in vampire blood.

**Josef.** Hessian's familiar and thrall since 1841, a thin, colorless young man who speaks only when instructed. He carries Hessian's terms in *Inheritance* and keeps the cairn after it.

**Dragomir.** Hessian's sire, a vampire of Vlad Dracula's court and still living. Enters in Book Six.

## PART ELEVEN: TERMINOLOGY

**Vampirism.** The condition of the undead who live on blood.

**The disease.** The ancient contagion carried in vampire saliva, fatal to humans within a day or two.

**The saving drink.** The single draught of the biter's blood that carries an infected person through death and back as a vampire.

**The Turning.** The whole passage from bite through death to return.

**Maker and progeny.** The vampire who turned another, and the vampire turned.

**Bloodline.** The lineage of makers and progeny.

**Maker bond.** The natural tie between maker and progeny.

**Blood-Mark.** An artificial blood-magic mechanism that enslaves a progeny's will to its maker.

**Familiar.** A human in an ongoing blood relationship with a vampire, without being turned.

**Thrall.** Anyone whose will has been gravely compromised by a vampire's control.

**Frenzy.** A passing state of extreme hunger in which conscious control fails.

**The wasting.** The Vanguard's term for the mutation brought on by living on animal blood alone.

**Gaunt.** A vampire that has completed the wasting: thin, dark grey, large-fanged, endlessly thirsty, and mindless. It cannot be saved.

**Mummification.** The dried, dormant state of a vampire deprived of all blood, from which human blood can revive it.

**Blood oath.** A promise bound by magic through blood.

**Via Noctis.** The vampire political faction of supernatural sovereignty.

**Period usage.** No one in the 1850s speaks of antibodies, viruses, or blood types. Harcourt speaks of a blood's "property" or "principle," of red and white "corpuscles," and of "contagion" and "disease."

## PART TWELVE: QUICK REFERENCE

### The Turning

A bite, or saliva in a wound, passes on the disease. Without the biter's blood, the victim dies within a day or two. With one drink of the biter's blood, the victim dies and returns as a vampire. The first hunger is severe, and control must then be learned. There is no halfway state.

### Feeding

Fresh human blood is best and necessary, drunk at least every few days. A willing donor by cut and cup is as good as a hunt and infects no one. Stored blood spoils within hours. Human blood mixed with animal blood keeps a vampire alive and sane but weak. Animal blood alone leads to the wasting within weeks to a couple of months, and a Gaunt at its end. No blood at all leads to mummification, from which human blood can wake the vampire. The blood of other vampires is poison. Ordinary food is taken only in small amounts and gives no real nourishment.

### Powers

Supernatural strength, bursts of great speed, and enhanced reflexes. Rapid healing and regeneration, fed by human blood. Keen hearing and scent, and good sight in low light. Hearing heartbeats at a distance, and seeing the halo of health and the haze of sickness. Reading emotion and lies from the body. Reduced pain, a life without age, and compulsion in varying strength. Rarer gifts come through bloodline or study, among them concealment, blood magic, influence over dreams, and territorial magic. Vampires cannot read minds or change shape.

### Weaknesses

Weaknesses that can kill: true sunlight beyond about thirty minutes, silver left in the body, fire reaching the heart, removal or destruction of the heart, and decapitation.

Weaknesses that disable: a stake through the heart, and silver wounds.

Weaknesses that are merely dangerous: hunger, heavy injury, and magical attack.

Things with no power: garlic, crosses by shape alone, running water, ordinary thresholds, mirrors, and artificial light.

### Confirming a Kill

A vampire is dead only when its heart or head is gone, or its heart has burned. A staked vampire is not dead. A mummified one is not dead. An ancient one is not dead until its magical defenses have been examined as well as its body.

## FINAL PRINCIPLE

Vampires in *Chronicles of the Shadow Vanguard* are neither simple monsters nor romantic immortals. They are people who died and came back to a changed body and a changed life. Blood keeps them alive, hunger tempts them, time estranges them, and power tempts them further. Their makers can teach them or enslave them. None of it removes choice, and that is what separates a vampire like Calum from a vampire like Hessian.

**The blood changes the body. The Turning changes the life. Neither decides the soul.**
