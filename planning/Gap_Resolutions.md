**How Hessian knew the family was inside (16.2, 16.3).** The default stands. **Hawick to Drumochter (8.6, 9.1).** The scene outline already answers it in 9.1: rail from Hawick to Edinburgh, then on to the Perth railhead, then hired horses up the military road over Drumochter. That is historically sound for December 1852. The Hawick line opened in 1849, Perth had been reachable by rail since 1848, and the line north of Perth did not open until 1856. Edinburgh stays a pass-through, as the 9.1 flag says. New decision: Percival has already ridden north out of Perth to meet the convoy before the party reaches Perth on the 7th, so they cross nobody there. He comes down the road to the ford from the convoy's side (9.4).

**The "Alex" rule (1.1).** Follow the Main Character Bible. "Alex" comes from Catherine always, and from Niall only in private, in danger, or in honesty. Edward says "my boy", Amelia "my darlings", the tenants "Master Alexander". Keep "Alex" out of 1.1 entirely, including Catherine's kitchen exchange, so that its first use lands in 1.2.

**Years of lessons (1.2, 8.3).** The lessons began in autumn 1845. That makes six years in autumn 1851 (1.2) and seven by December 1852 (8.3). Use "six" in Chapter 1.

## PART THREE: CORRECTIONS TO THE PACKETS' MOON DECISIONS

Moon positions were recomputed for Loch Maree (57.7°N, 5.45°W) and central Norfolk (52.7°N, 1.0°E) in local mean time. The method reproduced the outline's own moon times for 27 September 1851 (it gives 7:20 against "just after seven"), 4 October 1851 (12:35 against "about 12:30") and 23 December 1852 (5:17 against "about 5:12"). The new moons fall on 11 December 1852 and 9 January 1853.

**26.5 (13 January 1853, about eight at night, Norfolk).** **Keep the outline's "a thin moon."** The packet dropped it because it assumed new moon on the 10th or 11th. In fact the moon is four days old and 19 percent lit. At eight it hangs low over the western wall, about six degrees up in the west-southwest, and it sets about a quarter to nine. A thin crescent going down behind the wall is the image.

**17.2 (19 December 1852, about five past eleven).** Both the outline and the packet need a correction. The moon is waxing gibbous (65 percent, as the outline gives at 14.1). It is low in the west-southwest, about eighteen degrees up, not high in the south, and it sets about **half past one**, not quarter past twelve. Read "moonset" in 15.6 the same way: tonight's ritual is set before moonset at about half past one, and the first rescue disrupts it. The escape to the sea caves therefore runs under a setting moon until about half past one, then under starlight. No event changes.

**13.6 (18 December, about eleven at night).** The outline's "waxing gibbous" is right, just: the moon is 56 percent lit. It is low in the west-southwest, about nine degrees up. The packet's "half moon, low in the west-southwest" also reads true on the page. Either wording works. Drop the packet's parenthetical moonset times.

**14.1 (19 December, about three in the afternoon).** The outline is right: a waxing gibbous moon of about 62 percent, standing in the east-southeast, about sixteen degrees up. Use the outline's wording.

**11.1 (16 December, about three in the afternoon).** The packet is right: about a third lit, a thick crescent short of half, low in the south-southeast.

**12.6 (17 December, about ten at night).** The outline is right: a half moon low in the west-southwest, setting about eleven.

## PART FOUR: AUTHOR RULINGS

### Catherine's return and Inspector Pike (ruled by the author, 8 October 2026)

**The ruling.** The world learns Catherine is alive as soon as the family returns. The public account is that she was found walking on the family's Scottish land with no memory of the fourteen months. Because of her condition she is kept at home and receives no visitors. Pike is given this account and does not believe it. Through the series he investigates the family in secret, until a creature puts him in danger and they have to save him. He learns the truth then and turns from a thorn in their side into an ally.

**This supersedes the bibles on one point.** The Supporting Character Bible says Pike "is never read in, and chooses his own incuriosity," and the Series Bible says he "chooses to stop pulling a thread". Both now describe only his public stance in Book One. Proposed bible text: *Pike's "I'll simply stop reading it" is the official position, not the private one. He goes on investigating the Sterlings quietly through the series, until a creature attack from which the Vanguard saves him brings him the truth and makes him an ally.* Which book that happens in is still open.

**Book One, on the page:**

- **25.1.** Catherine still comes home separately, by night, in a closed carriage, which suits an invalid's seclusion. The staff are told that evening that Lady Catherine has been found and is unwell, and the news is in the village by morning. Drop the earlier default that only the inner circle knew. The inner household (O'Brien, Thomas, Mrs. Ashworth) still knows only what the public knows. Nothing in Book One reads them in.
- **25.3.** Pike receives two accounts. The deaths come from the Crown's Office: a fever contracted abroad, in a country left unnamed. Catherine's comes from the family: found on their Scottish land with no memory. Two authorities wrote two stories, and the seam between them shows. Pike does what the bible says he does and repeats the contradiction back exactly, once, without comment. For example: "Abroad," he says. "And Lady Catherine in Scotland." Nobody answers it. Then he delivers the outline's line unchanged: "I'll not close the file. I'll simply stop reading it." Through Alexander's eyes the line still reads as chosen incuriosity. Only a reader who already knows can see that it is a lie. Do not add anything else that tips it.
- Pike does not ask to see Catherine, and Alexander does not offer. Her seclusion is the answer before anyone asks.

### Lady Isobel Marchbank and Seraphina (accepted by the author, 8 October 2026; provisional, open to change during drafting)

**Isobel knew, and chose.** Anton Veress told Isobel that his blood was old, eastern and hunted. Letting the child pass as her late husband's was Isobel's own decision, made to hide Amelia, not something done to her. She was a partner in the secret.

**Seraphina carried the letters.** Seraphina never met Isobel. She carried letters between Anton and Isobel, sealed, and never read them. After the massacre she went to Edinburgh with no more than a name and a daughter's age to go on.

**The thread in Book Four.** The "first thread of the bloodline in Amelia's papers" is one of Isobel's replies to Anton. It came to Amelia after her mother died, and Amelia kept it without understanding it. When Catherine finds it, Seraphina knows the handwriting and the fold. She can say that she carried such letters, which is all her broken oath still allows. Harcourt's blood comparison then proves the rest.

**Book One.** None of this reaches the page. Isobel is never mentioned (Part Two, 13.3). Nothing in Book One may contradict it. In particular, Seraphina must never say or imply that she knew Isobel personally or read the letters.

**Bible note.** If this is confirmed, add it to the Supporting Character Bible entries for Lady Isobel Marchbank and Anton Veress, and to Seraphina's entry in the Main Character Bible, under the Oath.

### Catherine's place after Book One (ruled by the author, 8 October 2026)

Catherine lives at Sterling Castle in the care of Alexander, now Duke and head of the family. She receives no visitors and is kept out of sight while the search for a cure goes on. This is the arrangement Book One ends in (25.1, 25.7), and Book Two starts from it.

**How it fits the canon.** It agrees with the public account (an invalid without memory, kept at home) and with her night-only life. It also carries the tension the bibles already build into both siblings. Alexander's blind spot is that, "frightened for someone, he will override their choice" (Main Character Bible). Catherine's terms with Harcourt (25.2) are that "no one else consents for her". Her arc runs toward "adventure she chooses" (Series Bible). Being kept out of sight is where she starts, not where she ends. Book One shows the arrangement without resolving that tension.

**What society believes (ruled by the author, 8 October 2026).** Society is curious, sorry for her, and wrong. Since Catherine is seen by no one but family and staff, the rumor runs that the ordeal was too much for her and she has lost her wits. It is untrue. The family does not correct it, because it keeps people away, and that is all society knows. In Book One, word of her return reaches the village by morning (25.1). The rumors themselves belong after Book One. At most, a line of careful pity may surface where a scene already allows it, such as someone at Windsor asking after her (26.x). Add no scene or beat for it. The cost is Catherine's. A cover that calls her mad is the opposite of the self-possession her arc moves toward, and she knows it.
