# Inheritance: Story Beats and Plotlines
### Chronicles of the Shadow Vanguard, Book One

This is a working map of how *Inheritance* moves. It is built from the *Detailed Scene Outline*, the *Series Bible* (Parts Two, Nine, and Ten), and the character bibles, and it adds no canon of its own. Where it differs from the outline, the outline wins. Scene references use the outline's numbering: "10.3" means Chapter 10, Scene 3. Percentages are positions in the book, computed from the outline's chapter length guides, which total about 120,600 words.

---

## PART ONE: THE SPINE

**The book in one sentence.** A sheltered heir learns that his family has lied to him all his life, loses his parents rescuing a sister who has been turned into a vampire, and claims his inheritance not as an owner but as a protector, standing beside the friend who kept the biggest secret of all.

**The dramatic question.** Can Alexander get Catherine back from Hessian, and what will it cost him to become the man who can?

**Hook and climax as mirrors.** The Series Bible designs every book from its ending backward. Here the mirror is exact:

| At the Hook (Chapter 1) | At the Climax (Chapter 23) and close (Chapter 26) |
|---|---|
| Alexander is kept out of every room that matters. | He opens the covenant door with his own hand (22.3) and claims the chamber (23.4). |
| He waits to be allowed closeness. | He asks Niall to stand beside him (26.5). |
| He wants his father's approval. | He winds his father's watch himself (26.6). |
| Catherine longs to leave and cannot. | She refuses Hessian herself (23.2) and plans a route of her own (25.7). |
| The family runs on protective lies. | "No more rooms her son is kept out of" (13.3) becomes how he leads (21.1). |
| Hessian is welcomed to dinner. | Hessian's welcome is revoked: "an enemy of Crown and Kin" (23.4). |

## PART TWO: THE SEVEN POINTS

The Series Bible gives the target position of each point. The outline places them as follows.

| Point | Bible target | Chapter | Position | The beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–10% | 1–3 | 0–12% | The festival, Hessian's arrival, Catherine taken, the Grey Year of lies. |
| **Plot Turn One** | 10–15% | 4 | 12–15% | Two letters that contradict each other, Niall's "Yes," and the stair down into the hidden world. |
| **Pinch Point One** | 35–40% | 10 | 34–38% | "Kill the knife-thrower." Calum is compelled against Niall, and Alexander learns Niall has known for four years. |
| **Midpoint** | 50% | 13 | 46–50% | "She is a vampire." The family stops waiting and chooses to attack. |
| **Pinch Point Two** | 75–80% | 19 | 69–73% | Hessian's ultimatum, Beatrice's refusal, and Catherine's "The choice is mine." Alexander at the bottom. |
| **Plot Turn Two** | 80–85% | 20 | 73–77% | "She planted something." "The covenant responds to purpose." The signet goes into his breast pocket. |
| **Climax** | 85–100% | 22–24 | 81–92% | The second assault, the refusal, the throw, the claim, and Hessian driven into the sun. |
| **Resolution** | (within the climax band) | 25–26 | 92–100% | Home, the Crown, and "For Crown and Kin" spoken between equals. |

Four points land in their target bands. Pinch Point Two and Plot Turn Two arrive about six percent early, and the room they free goes to Chapters 20 to 22, where Alexander builds his own plan. That is a sound use of the space, but it makes Chapters 20 and 21 the most talk-heavy stretch in the back half (see Part Six).

## PART THREE: THE MAIN PLOT, PART BY PART

Each line gives what Alexander wants, the turn, and what it leaves him with.

### Part One: The Grey Year (Chapters 1–4, 0–15%)

1. **The Harvest Festival.** He wants an ordinary day. **Turn:** Hessian arrives at dusk and finds the one sentence that will open Catherine: "That sounds less like a life than a waiting room." **Leaves him:** uneasy, holding a feather too large for any Norfolk bird.
2. **The Price of Useless Land.** He wants to be trusted with his father's business. **Turn:** Niall knocks the ledger from the sill and breaks the compulsion. Edward refuses Harrow's Fen but freely sells the northern tract. That night Catherine is gone. **Leaves him:** knowing his father already knew which way to send men.
3. **The Grey Year.** He wants to find her, then to understand, then only to endure. **Turn:** The sighting letter, and his parents gone north by midnight without him. **Leaves him:** alone in a London square, sure he has been shut out.
4. **Letters Through the Storm.** He wants the truth. **Turn:** Two letters that contradict each other: Edward's forbids him to follow, and Amelia's says "Bring him." Then Niall's "Yes." **Leaves him:** at the top of the stair, betrayed and choosing to go down. *Plot Turn One.*

### Part Two: The Road North (Chapters 5–10, 15–38%)

5. **The Castle Beneath the Castle.** He wants to understand what his family has been. **Turn:** He calms the boggart on the stair by instinct and finds the Pryce file, the reason he was always alone. **Leaves him:** calmer, and more dangerous.
6. **Terms of Departure.** He wants to go north on terms he can accept. **Turn:** Seraphina's argument about the seal decides it. He goes under Niall's command, trading obedience in the field for honesty on demand. **Leaves him:** armed, afraid, and past the wards.
7. **The Smuggler's Road.** He wants to travel north unseen. **Turn:** The watcher on the platform, and Gideon's reroute over the Cheviots. **Leaves him:** by an inn fire, while the reader knows what Catherine has become and what Percival intends.
8. **First Blood.** He wants to survive the night. **Turn:** He kills a man on the stair before he can decide to, and the fire takes Catherine's diary. **Leaves him:** marked, and climbing into Scotland.
9. **The Capture Convoy.** He wants speed. **Turn:** He chooses to free the kelpie foal, his first plan, and Percival lets them go. **Leaves him:** proud, uncertain, and days behind. ("Would he?")
10. **The Frozen Crossing.** He wants to reach the loch. **Turn:** "Kill the knife-thrower." Calum turns the blow aside, and Alexander learns that Niall has known about Calum for four years. **Leaves him:** angry, still checking Niall's dressing himself. *Pinch Point One.*

### Part Three: Loch Maree (Chapters 11–13, 38–50%)

11. **The Price of Mercy.** He wants to return the foal and be recognized by his parents. **Turn:** Rhoswen accepts him, and Amelia names what the family did to him: "Control." **Leaves him:** at peace beside Niall, as his mother watches.
12. **Crown and Family.** He wants to be heard as a planner. **Turn:** He finds the timing flaw in Percival's offer, and Edward backs him. Calum sets the deadline. **Leaves him:** with half the recognition he wanted, and the clock running on Catherine's will.
13. **What the Water Keeps.** He wants the truth about Catherine, about Niall, and about his family. **Turn:** "She is a vampire." Seraphina's oath breaks and her eye goes dark. The council chooses not to wait. **Leaves him:** resolved, and trusted by his father. *Midpoint.*

### Part Four: The Refuge (Chapters 14–19, 50–73%)

14. **The One-Way Passage.** He wants to get into the refuge before the ritual. **Turn:** Rhoswen exposes Percival's override, and the water closes behind them. **Leaves him:** inside with no retreat, feeling the covenant answer him, and telling no one.
15. **The Ancient Refuge.** He wants to reach Catherine. **Turn:** The cages, then Catherine's face. **Leaves him:** holding his changed sister's plan.
16. **The First Rescue.** He wants to carry out her plan. **Turn:** He attacks before the signal. **Leaves him:** in a fight he started, with Niall down.
17. **The Fall of the Old Order.** He wants to save everyone. **Turn:** Edward holds the passage and dies. Amelia makes the safeguard and dies. Percival shuts the engine off early. **Leaves him:** at a sea cave at dawn, the signet in his fist.
18. **The Sea Caves.** He wants to hold what is left together. **Turn:** He asks Catherine instead of telling her, and finds his father's watch and the rifle ball. **Leaves him:** with one flaw corrected and his grief untouched.
19. **Moonset Terms.** He wants to keep Catherine. **Turn:** Hessian's terms arrive, Beatrice refuses, Alexander becomes his father at his worst, and Catherine answers: "The choice is mine." **Leaves him:** on the shingle with the signet unworn, and Niall's "I'll follow it." *Pinch Point Two.*

### Part Five: For Crown and Kin (Chapters 20–24, 73–92%)

20. **The Signet.** He wants a way to win that does not repeat his mistake. **Turn:** "She planted something," and "Purpose, not possession." **Leaves him:** steady for the first time, the signet in his breast pocket. *Plot Turn Two.*
21. **A Plan of His Own.** He wants a plan built from everyone's strengths. **Turn:** He does not take the head of the table. Catherine says "Watch me," and Rhoswen lays weed on his palm. **Leaves him:** watching his sister walk into the dark to her captor.
22. **The Second Assault.** He wants to reach the chamber in time and wait for her word. **Turn:** The door opens to his hand, and he spares Percival. **Leaves him:** waiting at the threshold. The flaw from 16.4 is corrected.
23. **For Crown and Kin.** He wants to protect everything in the chamber. **Turn:** Catherine refuses Hessian. "Throw, Nillo." Alexander claims the covenant to protect, not to own. **Leaves him:** climbing out through a burning house. *Climax.*
24. **Dawn on the Heather.** He wants the dawn to hold what the night won. **Turn:** The cairn takes Hessian, and Catherine's pull falls silent. Beatrice says "You'll do." **Leaves him:** on the shingle beside Niall, his hand at the breast pocket, steadying now rather than checking.

### Part Six: The Hidden Crown (Chapters 25–26, 92–100%)

25. **The House Without Them.** He wants to bring the family home and hold the house. **Turn:** The staff line forms behind him. Harcourt's finding. Pike's choice. **Leaves him:** with Catherine planning a route of her own, and the Institute at the walls.
26. **The Hidden Crown.** He wants to take up his place on terms he can live with. **Turn:** "For Crown and Kin," spoken between equals. **Leaves him:** with the watch wound, the ball still a mystery, and Niall beside him. Something waits in the north.

## PART FOUR: ALEXANDER'S INNER ARC

The outer plot is a rescue. The inner plot is a flaw corrected in stages. His blind spot, in the Main Character Bible, is that "frightened for someone, he will override their choice", the same flaw that made his parents lie to him.

| Stage | Scene | What he does |
|---|---|---|
| Kept out | 1–3 | Waits to be told. Accepts "duty" as an answer (2.4). |
| Chooses to know | 4.4–4.5 | Goes down the stair. |
| Acts by instinct | 5.4 | Kneels to the boggart with open hands. |
| Sets terms | 6.3 | Obedience in the field for honesty on demand. |
| Kills | 8.3 | His body acts before he decides. It costs him. |
| Makes a plan | 9.3 | "Then we don't leave it as it is." |
| Plans well | 12.3 | Finds the flaw, and is backed. |
| **Fails** | 16.4 | Attacks before the signal. His father and mother die in what follows. |
| Asks instead of tells | 18.3 | Offers Catherine the cup as a question. |
| **Falls back** | 19.3 | Under the pull, he becomes his father at his worst. |
| Is refused | 19.4 | "The choice is mine." |
| Understands purpose | 20.2 | "Purpose, not possession." |
| Leads from the side | 21.1 | Does not take the head of anything. |
| Lets her go | 21.6 | Watches Catherine walk to her captor. |
| Waits | 22.6 | Holds at the threshold for her word. |
| Claims to protect | 23.4 | The covenant answers. |
| Asks for an equal | 26.5 | "Beside me." |

The two falls, 16.4 and 19.3, keep this from being a straight climb. He gets better, fails catastrophically, gets better, and slips again before the change holds.

## PART FIVE: THE SUBPLOTS

Each subplot lists what it is about, its beats in Book One, how it ends in this book, and what it hands to the series.

### A. Alexander and Niall (the romance)

**What it's about.** Six years of knife lessons, practical care that neither man names, and the biggest secret in the book, which Niall keeps. The Series Bible fixes its movement for Book One: from unspoken friendship to chosen equality, with no confession.

**Beats.** The private hour in the training yard, with Catherine seeing what Alexander won't admit (1.2). The feather, and "Nothing you need to carry tonight" (1.5). February, and an honest answer (3.4). "Yes" (4.4). Niall opens the door without anyone's leave (4.5). "I'll not decide for you again what you're fit to carry" (5.6). Terms in the yard (6.3). The draught from the door (7.6). "The first one should cost you" (8.5). **Rupture:** "Four years" (10.4). He still checks the dressing himself (10.6). Amelia sees them and lets the tent flap fall (11.6). The word "loved" (13.2). The Gaelic notebook, "Things I'll not say out loud" (13.6). A hand on his back (15.4). Niall takes the blade (16.6). The scar, bound with Catherine's handkerchief (18.2). "I'll follow it" (19.6). Alexander keeps watch beside him (20.6). He respects Niall's choice (21.4). "Throw, Nillo" (23.3). Side by side (24.6). Niall laughs in Old Town Scots (25.6). The exchange (26.4). The handkerchief falls (26.5).

**How it ends in Book One.** As equals, unspoken.
**Hands to the series.** The edge of speech in Book Four. Alexander speaks first in Book Six. In Book Five, Silas holds a letter proving what Alexander feels.

### B. Catherine: from dreamer to survivor

**What it's about.** A girl who wanted any life but a waiting room gets the adventure she wanted, in a body that cannot stand in the sun. Her interludes give the book its second pulse and the reader its dramatic irony.

**Beats.** The window gaze (1.1). "Can you imagine?" (1.4). The waiting-room line (1.5). "Duty," and her laugh (2.4). The locked room (2.5). The atlas gone and the pin money left behind (3.2). **Interlude:** Calum tells her to keep hold of something of her own, and the swallow marks begin (3.3). **Interlude:** the cup, and she lets "Elisabeta" pass (7.3). **Interlude:** Calum let the bark fall on purpose (10.5). "She is a vampire" (13.1). **Interlude:** she leaves the atlas behind (14.5). "Don't look at me as though I've already been buried" (15.6). Her plan (16.1). The safeguard (17.3). She carries Niall through the water (17.5). He asks (18.3). **Interlude:** what she carries, and she protects it (18.6). The pull (19.3). "The choice is mine" (19.4). Her terms (20.1). She accepts his apology lightly (20.3). "Watch me" (21.2). Pearl powder (21.6). **Interlude:** at the gate (22.1). "Wilhelm" (23.2). "It's gone quiet" (24.4). "Lady Catherine" (25.2). **Interlude:** the jar, and she laughs (25.7).

**Rhythm.** Her viewpoint returns every three or four chapters (3, 7, 10, 14, 18, 22, 25), so the reader never loses her for long. This is one of the outline's strongest design choices.
**How it ends in Book One.** At home, kept out of sight, the atlas open to the Danube, and planning out loud a route she will travel by night. Society believes she has lost her wits, a cover that is the opposite of the self-possession she has won (Gap Resolutions, Part Four).
**Hands to the series.** The cure. The returning pull as Hessian revives (Books Three to Five). The bloodline thread in Amelia's papers (Book Four).

### C. The family that runs on secrets (Edward and Amelia)

**What it's about.** Parents who protected their son by lying to him, and what that protection costs. This subplot makes the rescue tragic rather than merely dangerous.

**Beats.** Edward puts Hessian's letter away unread (1.3). The ledger, and the sale (2.1). Amelia's ink darkens (2.2). Edward refuses to explain (2.3). He sends men north within minutes (2.5). The foreign-suitor story told to Pike (3.1). Gone by midnight (3.5). "Bring him," against Edward's order not to follow (4.3). Amelia's gloves named (5.1). The reunion (11.4). "Control" (11.5). The partial seal explained (12.2). Edward backs his son (12.3). "I ought to have asked" (13.4). "Good. Watching you stand here asking me the harder question" (14.1). "Protect. Not own" (17.1). The safeguard (17.3). The flood takes Edward (17.4). The watch and the ball (18.4). "She planted something" (20.1). His father's sentence (20.5). "She planted it" (23.2). He winds the watch (26.6).

**How it ends in Book One.** Both die on the page, actively and purposefully. Edward passes on the signet and a purpose; Amelia leaves a safeguard her son must earn the right to wake.
**Hands to the series.** The rifle ball (Book Six). Edward's black-cord packet (Book Four). Amelia's papers and silver-lined dressings (Book Four).

### D. Calum: the compelled betrayer

**What it's about.** Niall's near-brother, Turned and Blood-Marked by Hessian in 1845. He is the book's proof that a transformed being can choose good, and the mirror of Alexander's own move from being directed to choosing.

**Beats.** The raptor at the garden wall, unseen (1.5). Teaching Catherine to hold on to herself (3.3). The raptor recognized (10.1). "Kill the knife-thrower," and "Colie" (10.3). The bark was dropped on purpose (10.5). "She's learning" (12.6). Bringing the timing (16.1). "He's felt me reaching" (16.2). The blade lands on Niall's ribs, not his throat (16.6). At the gate (22.1). "Throw, Nillo," and "Do as he asks" (23.3). The silver is drawn (23.6). Laid in the shallows (24.3). The binder's hands (25.6).

**How it ends in Book One.** The Mark broken with silver and its scar remaining, mending books in the sanctuary beneath the castle.
**Hands to the series.** His family in Edinburgh (Book Two). His werewolf nature (Book Three). Taken by the Institute (Book Five).

### E. Rhoswen's mercy debt

**What it's about.** One act of mercy, chosen at a real cost in time, that comes back as the decisive help at the climax. It is the book's argument that kindness to a creature is strategy, not weakness.

**Beats.** The convoy (9.2). The hitch in her stride, and the choice (9.3). The ford (9.4). The price in lost days (9.6). Beag saves Ewan unasked (10.2). Beag lifts her head at the still water (11.1). Rhoswen touches his temple and throat (11.2). Her terms, and Gideon gives up his iron token (11.3). She strikes at Percival and exposes the override (14.2). Asking, and weed laid on his palm (21.3). The wall of water (23.5). Out through the water (23.6). The touch repeated: the debt honored (24.3).

**How it ends in Book One.** Paid in full, both ways.
**Hands to the series.** Ewan as warden of Loch Maree.

### F. Percival and the Institute

**What it's about.** The human antagonist, whose schemes always partly succeed. He gives the book its second clock and its political future.

**Beats.** The Ashcroft name and the lecture season (3.5). The watcher at York (7.4). **Interlude:** "Observation can be conducted at close range" (7.5). Higgins: "The knife-thrower from the cave" (8.2). The improved machinery (9.2). Percival arrives (9.4). He lets them go and lights a flare (9.5). His offer, and its flaw (12.3). The override exposed (14.2). The engine (16.5). **Interlude:** he shuts it off early (17.2). The machine on the stair, and Alexander spares him (22.4). **Interlude:** "Sterling," said with respect (24.2). The watcher's boots at the fen wall (25.4).

**How it ends in Book One.** He escapes with rubbings of one line of the seven sigils, now respecting Alexander as a dangerous enemy.
**Hands to the series.** Manchester and the artificial sun (Book Two). Higgins as a source (Book Two). Will Pryce as Percival's agent (Book Three).

### G. Vanguard politics: Beatrice and Sebastian

**What it's about.** The institution Alexander inherits does not want him yet. Beatrice holds command and makes him earn every inch. Sebastian argues against him, and in the end works against him.

**Beats.** "He has proposed nothing at all yet, Sebastian" (5.2). The ethic, stated as a question (5.3). The Pryce file (5.5). The debate on departure (6.4). The party from the sea, and her rebuke (12.1). The council (13.1, 13.5): Beatrice chooses not to wait. She knows everything with one look (18.1). Josef's terms on the shingle (19.1). **How it ends in Book One.** Alexander has her people, not her command. Sebastian has already written to someone in the south.
**Hands to the series.** Formal confirmation (Book Two). Earned command, and Sebastian exposed (Book Four).

### H. Seraphina's oath

**What it's about.** A witch bound by a dead man's oath, whose silence at the festival let the sale go through. Her confession is the cost of magic shown on the page.

**Beats.** Her eye silvers at dinner and she says nothing (1.4). **Interlude, Amelia's:** the ink, and a precise truth that tells nothing (2.2). She answers an adjacent question (6.2). Her argument about the seal decides the departure (6.4). **The confession:** her eye goes dark (13.3). "One of seven" (15.3). **Interlude:** the passage, and endurance (15.5). She tends Niall's wound (18.2). "She planted something" (20.1). "Purpose, not possession" (20.2). The door behind the relief (22.3). She feels the water rising (22.5). She draws the silver (23.6). At the exchange (26.4).

**How it ends in Book One.** Unforgiven by Amelia, who dies first. Becoming Alexander's mentor.
**Hands to the series.** Silas at the massacre (Book Three). The bloodline (Book Four). The Heart of Morven, and her death protecting Catherine (Book Five).

### I. Gideon Wren: loyalty freely given

**What it's about.** A smuggler who pays debts he could walk away from. His loyalty is never bought, which is why it is worth having.

**Beats.** The vault beneath the stables (6.4). Through the wall (6.6). He pays for Davey Coop when he thinks no one is looking (7.1). He reroutes them (7.4). **How it ends in Book One.** He refuses payment, and goes home to "a boy to see about."

### J. Ewan Macrae and the packet

**What it's about.** The prequel's frightened boy is now a courier with Edward's sealed packet and a kelpie's trust.

**Beats.** The gatehouse bell (4.2). Through the wall (6.6). Beag, and naming her (9.3). His leg breaks at the ford (9.4). Beag saves him (10.2). He stays with the horses (14.1). **Interlude:** no knocks, and the packet's terms become his last order (18.5). The debt honored (24.3).

**How it ends in Book One.** He stays at Loch Maree.
**Hands to the series.** He is warden of the loch. The three knocks and the opened packet come in Book Four.

### K. The home front: Thomas, Pike, and Harcourt

**What it's about.** The world the family must keep from finding out, and the science that might one day undo the bite. These scenes pull the book home at measured intervals, so the reader feels what is at stake in the ordinary world.

**Beats.**
- **Thomas Barrow:** Harrow's Fen (1.1). The door (4.1). **Interlude:** the gatehouse (6.5). **Interlude:** "Easy now" (12.5). The steward (25.1). The fen wall (25.4).
- **Pike:** the fresh plaster (3.1). London (3.5). He watches the wagons leave (6.5). He lets the boggart child go (12.5). "I'll simply stop reading it" (25.3). By the author's ruling, he goes on investigating in secret (Gap Resolutions, Part Four).
- **Harcourt:** "Catherine" (6.1). **Interlude:** the microscope (12.4). "Lady Catherine," and Catherine's terms (25.2). The exchange (26.4).

**How it ends in Book One.** The house held, Pike unconvinced, and the cure begun.
**Hands to the series.** John and Mary Barrow (Book Two). Pike becomes an ally once a creature attack forces the truth. The cure runs through every book.

### L. Will and Samuel Pryce

**What it's about.** Why Alexander grew up lonely. The butler's son, his only childhood friend, vanished in 1841 because his father was spying for Ashcroft.

**Beats.** The pantry door he passes without looking at (1.1). The Pryce file (5.5).
**Hands to the series.** Will returns as Percival's agent in Book Three, and Alexander knows him on sight.

**What the map shows.**

- **No chapter carries fewer than three subplots.** Even the thinnest, Chapter 19, braids the main plot with three side threads, because it is the bottom of the book and narrows on purpose.
- **The romance and Catherine's arc are the two continuous currents,** present in nearly every chapter. Everything else comes and goes in measured waves.
- **The antagonists alternate.** Percival carries the pressure while Hessian is off the page (Chapters 7 to 12), and Calum carries Hessian's will by proxy until Hessian himself returns (Chapters 16 and 22 to 24).
- **The home front goes quiet from Chapter 13 to Chapter 24.** That is deliberate: once the family is underground, the ordinary world drops away, and its return in Chapter 25 makes the homecoming land.

## PART SEVEN: KEEPING IT DYNAMIC

This book does not have a basic plot. It has about twelve interwoven subplots, each set up and paid off on the page. Its protagonist's arc includes two real failures. It has a second viewpoint returning on a steady rhythm and an antagonist who partly wins. The risk is not that the story is thin. The risk is that a few stretches could sag in the drafting if their quiet scenes run long.

**Strengths to protect in the draft.**

1. **Reversals in every Part.** Each Part has at least one turn that makes things worse for Alexander because of his own choice: the sale (2), the kill (8), the rupture (10), the attack before the signal (16), the slip under the pull (19).
2. **Interludes as dramatic irony.** The reader knows what Catherine has become (7.3) a full six chapters before Alexander does (13.1). Every scene in between plays against that knowledge.
3. **Mercy as strategy.** Choosing Beag (9.3) costs days and pays off at the climax (23.5). This is the book's moral argument, made through plot rather than speech.
4. **Flaw and correction mirrored.** The attack at 16.4 and the wait at 22.6 are the same moment, played twice.

**Watch-points for drafting** (no outline changes, only cautions):

1. **Chapters 5 and 6, the institution revealed** (15–23%). Two chapters of explanation follow straight after Plot Turn One. The outline builds in friction (Sebastian in 5.2, the boggart in 5.4, Harcourt in 6.1, the debate in 6.4). Keep every explanatory scene an argument, per the bible's rule, and let the boggart scene run hot.
2. **Chapters 14 to 17, underground** (50–66%). This is about sixteen percent of the book in one continuous place, with two scenes the outline itself marks as turnless: 14.3, "This is atmosphere," and 15.5, "This is endurance." Draft those two short, and let the bell (14.6) and the missing twin carving (15.1) carry the dread.
3. **Chapters 20 and 21, the plan** (73–81%). Pinch Point Two and Plot Turn Two come early, so these chapters are mostly talk: terms, an apology, Beatrice's price, the council, a letter. The clock (moonset on the 23rd) must be felt in every scene. 21.6, Catherine putting on pearl powder to walk to her captor, is the pressure release to build toward.
4. **Chapters 24 to 26, the long close** (89–100%). There are nineteen scenes after the climax, many of them planting threads for later books. Each one should still turn something for Alexander personally, as most already do ("You'll do," "Lady Catherine," "I'll simply stop reading it," "Beside me"). If any reads as a sequel teaser, trim it in revision.

**Optional ideas, if more texture is wanted later.** These are suggestions only. None is canon, and none is needed.

- **A Davey Coop callback.** Gideon's boy from 7.1 pays off in 25.5 ("a boy to see about"). One line in 15.2, as Gideon chooses to stay with the captives, could let Alexander connect the two.
- **A second Pike sighting in the Grey Year.** Pike already appears at 3.1, 3.5, and 6.5. One line in 3.4, Pike walking past the gate on his fortnightly round (Supporting Bible), would make his secret investigation feel continuous from the start.
- **Thomas between 12 and 25.** Thomas's growth into steward lands at 25.1 and 25.4. A single line of a letter from Thomas, read at the camp or the bothy, would keep the home front alive in the reader's mind through the underground stretch. Only consider this if the gap feels long in the draft.
