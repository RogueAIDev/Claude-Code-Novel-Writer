## Outline Assessment

### Overall Structure: **Strong, but the packet is incomplete for drafting**

**What's working:**
- The scene has a clear want (Niall's whole attention), a clear obstacle (he is watching something else), and a clean turn (Catherine makes it three).
- It plants three objects in one short scene: the practice knife, the diagram, and the handkerchiefs.
- Niall's first appearance is fully specified: age, height, coloring, scars, dress, voice, and tics.
- Continuity out is right: the rising wind brings the overcast that lets Hessian arrive at dusk in 1.3.

**Missing elements** (things a writer must know and the packet doesn't say):
1. **No weapons rule.** Live steel first comes in 6.3, so these must still be the blunt iron practice knives. The packet never says so.
2. **What Niall sees in the tree line.** The packet says only "never stated." The prose can keep it unstated, but the writer needs to know what is there so Niall's behavior, the timing, and the reader's clues stay consistent.
3. **How Niall addresses Alexander when Catherine arrives.** The bible gives Niall "Alex" only "in private, danger, or honesty." When Catherine arrives, the scene stops being private. What does he call Alexander then?
4. **How the lessons end now.** The prequel's first lesson ended with "Tomorrow?" / "Tomorrow, then." The packet calls it a ritual, but doesn't say whether it is still spoken at the end of every lesson.
5. **Where the blunt knife came from.** Which knife Alexander kept, and whether Niall knows it is gone.
6. **Carry-over from 1.1 Draft 2.** The splinter in Alexander's right hand (his throwing hand) and the rooks in the chestnuts.
7. **Throwing distance after six years.** In 1845 the chalk line was twelve feet.

**Things that do not work and need fixing:**
1. **"Seven years learning to read that pause"** (outline summary).
   a. The lessons began in autumn 1845, so it is six years. Already settled in Gap Resolutions. Use "six."
2. **The outline says "Alex" appears here "only from Niall and Catherine."**
   a. This is right, but the outline's 1.1 rule ("from family and Niall") contradicts it. Already corrected in Gap Resolutions, Part Five.

**Issues you may be holding on to:**
1. **"Master Alexander" for a duke's heir.** *Not for this scene; a series question.* Historically, the eldest son of a duke in 1851 would bear one of his father's lesser titles by courtesy (for example, "the Marquess of ___") and be called "my lord" by servants and tenants. "Master" was for boys and for the sons of gentry. The prequel and the outline use "Master Alexander" throughout, so it is established canon. Readers who know the period will notice it.
   A. Keep "Master Alexander" as a deliberate Sterling household custom. One line somewhere in the book could make that explicit (an old family habit, from when the heir was a boy).
   B. Give Alexander a courtesy title from Book One on. This would need a change to the prequel or a note on it.
   C. Leave it, and accept the inaccuracy.
   *This doesn't block 1.2, where Niall's public address can be "sir" either way. It does need deciding before Chapter 2's dinner guests and Chapter 3's Pike.*

## Research Recommendations for 1.2

### Facts fixed by the prequel (the scene must match these)
- **The knives** are iron, dull-edged, slightly heavier through the hilt than a fighting blade, about the length of hand and forearm, with a broad blunt point and a leather-wrapped grip. There were six, laid in a straight line on a narrow table and kept in a wooden box. One bore old nicks from striking stone.
- **The target** is a painted circle on the wood, with a dark knot near the center. The Location Bible makes it the butt of the old chestnut.
- **The rules:** throw only north. "If I say stop, you stop." Never catch a blade by the point. Never turn with a knife in your hand.
- **The technique:** the half-turn throw. "Elbow leads. Wrist stays quiet. Power from the shoulder."
- **The touch:** in 1845, Niall corrected Alexander's elbow *without touching him*, using the flat of another knife. **This is the most important fact for 1.2.** By 1851 he corrects the wrist with his own hand and takes it away too soon. Six years have turned no touch into a touch withdrawn. The scene should let the reader feel that this is not how it started.
- **How they talk:** dry, deadpan, quick ("If it does, I shall revise the lesson." "It has reason to be attentive."). Niall laughs rarely, and his laugh is "brief, warm, and immediately cut short."
- **The first lesson ended:** Alexander asked "Tomorrow?" and Niall said "Tomorrow, then."

### Issues with current continuity
- **Address forms, 1845:** Niall used "sir" and "Master Alexander." Alexander used "Mr. Fraser." By 1851 the private forms are "Alex" (Niall, in private) and, presumably, "Niall" (Alexander). Neither the packet nor the bibles say what Alexander calls Niall.
- **1.1 Draft 2:** Alexander worked a splinter out of the heel of his **right** hand at about eleven. At two he is throwing with it.

### Data points about readers and the genre
- Slow-burn queer historical romance readers (KJ Charles's readership, which the style guide names) look for *competence intimacy*: one character teaching another a physical skill, with touch that is functional and loaded at once. This scene is the book's purest example, so it should be written for that pleasure, not past it.
- The genre's readers also notice forms of address. A name withheld in company reads as intimacy protected, not intimacy denied.

### Counterarguments
- *"Show what Niall sees in the trees."* No. The outline's design is that the reader, like Alexander, sees only Niall seeing. Keep the cause off the page. The writer still needs to know it (Decision 2).
- *"Have Catherine say what she sees."* No. Her bible lists it among her secrets. She watches, and says nothing.

## Numbered Decisions

**Decision 1. Weapons.**
- A. Blunt iron practice knives from the prequel's set, still. Live steel waits for 6.3. *Recommended.* (Required by canon.)

**Decision 2. What is in the tree line (never stated on the page).**
- A. **Rooks go up out of the trees beyond the water-meadow, twice,** as something moves among them. This ties to the rooks in 1.1. The cause, never revealed in Book One, is **Josef**, Hessian's human familiar, scouting the house by daylight, which a vampire cannot do. He is on the carriage box at dusk (1.3). *Recommended.*
- B. Nothing visible. Niall's instinct alone.
- C. An Institute watcher. *Not recommended:* it muddies Hessian's evening, and the Institute's siege belongs to later chapters.

**Decision 3. Names when Catherine arrives.**
- A. Niall says "Alex" while they are alone. **The moment Catherine is in earshot, he goes back to "sir,"** and the private name is withdrawn exactly as his hand is. Catherine hears the change. Alexander calls him "Niall" alone, and "Fraser" in company. *Recommended.*
- B. Niall keeps "Alex" in front of Catherine, as family.

**Decision 4. "Tomorrow, then."**
- A. The exchange still closes every lesson: Alexander says "Tomorrow?" and Niall says "Tomorrow, then." It closes 1.2, after Catherine has gone. Chapter 26 (26.5) then becomes the last time the old ritual is spoken. *Recommended.*
- B. Leave it to Chapter 26 alone.

**Decision 5. The kept knife.**
- A. It is the knife from the prequel's first good throw, the one that stuck below the center ring. Niall noticed it missing from the box six years ago, has never mentioned it, and has never replaced it, so **the box still holds five.** Alexander sees the gap every lesson. Neither speaks of it. *Recommended.* (One line. The reader understands both silences.)
- B. The set was replaced and nobody counts.

**Decision 6. The splinter.**
- A. When Niall corrects his grip, he sees the torn skin at the heel of Alexander's hand, says nothing, and moves his own thumb to the other side of the grip so it doesn't press there. Alexander notices that he noticed. *Recommended.* (Care as action, per the style guide.)
- B. Ignore it.

**Decision 7. Distance.**
- A. Seven paces now, about twenty feet. Niall paced it out himself, and says the exact figure if asked (his measuring tic). *Recommended.*
