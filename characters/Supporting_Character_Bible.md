## Allies of Loch Maree

### Rhoswen

**The Loch Maree kelpie matriarch · nonhuman ally**

**Appearance.** In water-horse form she has a great scaled neck and a mane of weed and coarse black strands. Her human form is a woman who looks neither young nor old, with sharp, strange features. She is beautiful only in the way a Highland storm is, without gentleness or invitation. Her eyes stay silver, and fine scales trace her temples and the backs of her hands. Her hair is dark and reed-tangled. Silver burns marked her palms after the Ashcroft chain.

**Speech.** Almost none. She speaks exactly one clear human line in the prequel, "Not the little ones," and true speech is reserved for moments of the greatest stakes. Otherwise she communicates through water itself: ripples that answer, a rising chill, a current pulling a boat off course, the loch going still.

**Details.** Once, Edward knelt unarmed before her, and she pressed her clawed fingertips to the old scars at his temple and then to the pulse in his throat. That is her memory of the family, and she gives Alexander the same touch at their first meeting and again when the debt is honored. She is never a mount or a pet. Her help is chosen and limited. She is a descendant of Eithne, the kelpie queen who co-founded the covenant, though the Sterlings do not learn this until late in the series. Her mother's testimony, recorded in cipher in Alasdair Fraser's notebook, holds the method for the further agent of the cure.

### Rhoswen's Foals

**Two in the prequel, one grown in Book One**

**Appearance.** Pale blue-grey scales, no larger than young Highland ponies in 1845. The smaller one favored a foreleg, and Percival marked the injury for valuation. Grown by 1852, she still carries the old hitch in her stride when tired, which is how Alexander recognizes her in Percival's convoy. She shifts between scale and skin.

**Speech.** None.

**Details.** The larger foal is *Liath* ("grey") and the smaller, formerly injured one is *Beag* ("little one"), the name Ewan's people give her, which also echoes her mother's one line. Beag is the foal taken in the Book One convoy and freed at the Dalwhinnie ford. On the loch shore at sunrise after the climax, she comes out of the water to stand beside her mother.

### Ewan Macrae

**Highland courier, later the Vanguard's warden of Loch Maree · 17 in the prequel, 24 in Book One**

**Appearance.** At seventeen: pale beneath the dirt, trembling hands, his cap pulled off in rooms that intimidate him. At twenty-four he is weather-hardened and lean from long riding, with sandy-brown hair, cheeks reddened by wind, a rider's bowed ease in the saddle, and a cap still pulled low.

**Speech.** A soft Highland burr, plain and practical, respectful around rank but no longer paralyzed by it. He calls Alexander "sir," turns his cap once in his hands when he's nervous, and uses Rhoswen's name without noticing he has done it.

**Details.** He carries, every day, the second packet Edward gave him in 1845: no address, tied with black cord, to be opened only if three evenly spaced knocks come to his door after dark on a night when no one has sent for him. He has carried it "the same as I've carried his good opinion of me." The knocks come in Book Four. The packet holds Edward's letter naming Ewan the Vanguard's warden of Loch Maree, and Edward's notes identifying the cave carving as one of seven. In *Inheritance* he rides through the storm with the letters, is hurt on the road north and rides on with his leg splinted, and refuses to go home while His Grace's sister is somewhere he might be of use. His grandfather worked the kelp trade and knew Josiah Webb.

## Vanguard, Crown, and Road

### Dr. Miriam Harcourt

**Vanguard physician, holder of the Vanguard's medical charter**

**Appearance.** Mid-forties, compact and sharp, with spectacles, quick unsentimental fingers, and the economy of a woman who catalogues a person the way she would a specimen. Dark hair scraped back and going grey at the parting, sleeves always pushed to the elbow, a leather notebook at her side.

**Speech.** Exact medical vocabulary in the period's terms, brisk and unsentimental: the blood's "property" or "principle," red and white "corpuscles," "contagion" and "disease." She delivers hard news without softening it.

**Details.** Alexander first meets her before the journey north, at her table, examining a brush carrying Catherine's blood. While he is in the north she finds in that blood a property she cannot identify, one that suggests the disease might one day be driven back, and that will not work without a further agent. She tells him when he returns, and writes the terms down. Her clinical detachment traces to a past supernatural patient she treated as a specimen, a memory Catherine's insistence on personhood forces open. She distinguishes four degrees of hope: suppression, stabilization, reversal, and a true cure. Her first attempt at the further agent fails in Book Two, and in Book Four she proves the bloodline by setting Alexander's blood beside Catherine's and testing the Loch Maree dressings Dr. Hale kept from 1845.

### Gideon Wren

**Smuggler and information broker, human**

**Appearance.** A narrow, quick man in his thirties with a clever, ordinary face built to be forgotten, ink ground permanently into the creases of his right hand, and a good coat worn badly on purpose.

**Speech.** Fast, irreverent, transactional, and openly mercenary about his own honesty: "A man who tells you exactly what he wants is usually lying about something else entirely." He is stung by any suggestion that his word is bad, and for a moment it shows.

**Details.** He forges manifests on the spot in a hand that varies its slant letter by letter, so no two entries match. He spins a stamped metal token on the table while he thinks. He runs Alexander's land route north, by cattle van, drovers' road, and smuggler's path. He quietly pays for Davey Coop through the winter. His loyalty is eventually given freely rather than bought.

### Captain Ellery

**Vanguard field officer**

**Appearance.** A weathered woman near forty, broad through the shoulders, with close-pinned hair and a man's riding coat cut down to fit.

**Speech.** Clipped, military, economical. She speaks in distances and hours.

**Details.** She leads the operatives of Beatrice's official party, which goes north by sea, and at the second assault her people light the field and take the gate road by force. As one of the few senior women in the field, she is a standing rebuke to Sebastian's prejudice.

### Queen Victoria

**Formal head of the Shadow Vanguard · born 1819, 33 in 1852**

**Appearance.** A little under five feet, made larger by the economy of her presence, in plain dark silk with a single mourning brooch at her throat. In the winter of 1852 the court is in mourning for the Duke of Wellington, who died that September, which gives the brooch and dark silk a public reason beyond the Sterlings.

**Speech.** Formal and exact when speaking as the Crown. Otherwise dry and unhurried, and she never needs to raise her voice.

**Details.** She receives Alexander at Windsor Castle on 11 January 1853. She delivers the public account of Edward and Amelia's deaths herself, "a fever contracted abroad," and calls it near enough to the truth's shape. She confirms Beatrice as Acting Commander. Prince Albert, read into the Vanguard as her consort and its quiet patron of science, is revealed as such in Book Four.

### Inspector Elias Pike

**Norwich police inspector**

**Appearance.** About forty, heavy-lidded, with a greying moustache and a plain brown coat too warm for the room. His manner is too unhurried for the occasion, and he carries a notebook he rarely consults because he remembers everything.

**Speech.** Patient questions, exact repetition of contradictions, and he never raises his volume to match anyone else's. "I'll not close a file on a missing young woman because a family finds the details inconvenient."

**Details.** He writes twice monthly through the Grey Year, whether or not he has anything to report, and walks past the castle gate twice a month. He sets down a question the way a man sets down a stone he intends to pick up again. He is never read in, and chooses his own incuriosity. When he calls in January 1853 he tells Alexander, "I'll not close the file. I'll simply stop reading it." Ivor Vane's firm, which goes on paying the rates on Hessian's burned tract, is the loose thread he chooses not to pull.

## Sterling Household and Estate

### Mr. O'Brien

**Senior castle steward**

**Appearance.** Sixty, spare, silver-haired, and immaculately pressed, with the stillness of a man who has spent forty years not being noticed on purpose.

**Speech.** Precise and formal, hard to surprise. He receives instructions rather than explanations.

**Details.** He rarely smiles in public, but the staff read a faint, unreadable cheer in his stillness. His expression gives away exactly nothing. He has no independent arc.

### Thomas Barrow

**Under-steward, acting steward beneath O'Brien**

**Appearance.** In his forties, big-handed, fair and freckled from outdoor work, wearing a coat too formal for the work he does in it.

**Speech.** A respectful local Norfolk voice that grows more direct with responsibility, carrying real weight beneath a light delivery: "Most estates, a man goes lame, he goes without wage the same week."

**Details.** His father, John Barrow, works the hay cart on a ruined knee, kept on by the family, and his mother, Mary, keeps house in the village; both appear in Book Two. He knows "Easy now" works on frightened stable boys, and uses it on the boggart child too. He tells Alexander that Harrow's Fen feeds eleven families this winter. He carries the home-front interludes and grows from acting steward into a steward of the whole community.

### Mrs. Thompson

**Castle cook**

**Appearance.** Broad, floury, red-forearmed, in her fifties, with her hands folded hard in her apron when she's decided not to cry.

**Speech.** Warm, loud, practical. Everyone is "love." "I cook the meals, love. I don't interrogate dukes before breakfast." Her voice carries into the yard when she's losing an argument.

**Details.** She refused to strip the festival ribbons before first frost. Her kitchen is the book's counterweight to cold stone.

### Mrs. Ashworth

**Housekeeper of the hidden accounts and keeper of the sanctuary entrance**

**Appearance.** Neat, fortyish, keys at her waist, a housekeeper's black with a pencil pinned in her hair. Her face goes the particular grey of a woman doing rapid, unhappy arithmetic.

**Speech.** Brisk, the same voice for linen counts and creature feeding.

**Details.** She keeps the supernatural accounts with the same competence as the linens. She reads the boggart family's clicks and translates them for Thomas and Harcourt.

### Dr. Hale

**Ordinary household physician**

**Appearance.** An elderly country doctor, round-spectacled and kindly, forever slightly out of breath.

**Speech.** Fussy, well-meaning, full of questions nobody answers.

**Details.** He is kept permanently ignorant, the family's running joke. He drew a sharp breath at Amelia's palms after Loch Maree and, out of a physician's habit of keeping what puzzles him, kept the dressings. They become evidence in Book Four.

### Mr. Hollis

**The family's man of business**

**Appearance.** Thin, sandy, an ink-stained cuff, and a clerk's stoop.

**Speech.** Quiet and deferential, fond of numbers.

**Details.** He moves between the London townhouse and the castle estate office with the ledgers, which is why he turns up at both.

### Bess

**Catherine's maid**

**Appearance.** Nineteen, round-faced, a Norfolk village girl with red, chapped hands.

**Speech.** Quick, giggling, and fiercely loyal to Catherine. Her voice carried the length of the east corridor when she found the locked door at dawn on 5 October 1851.

**Details.** She was the one person who knew about Catherine's pin-money jar. She knew it existed, not what it was for, and that makes her a quiet witness when Alexander pieces together his sister's secret.

### Simmons and Carrow

**Estate men**

**Appearance.** Simmons has a gamekeeper's build, bearded and taciturn. Carrow is younger, wiry, and a fast rider.

**Speech.** Few words from either.

**Details.** Edward sent them quietly up the northern road in their own coats on the night of the abduction. Carrow is the Widow Carrow's son.

### The Fen Boggart Child

**A sheltered creature of Harrow's Fen**

**Appearance.** Child-sized and mud-brown, with amber eyes, narrow hunched shoulders, long-fingered hands, and a pelt like wet reeds.

**Speech.** None in human words. It clicks like a snipe when it's frightened, and its family speaks in clicks that Mrs. Ashworth can read.

**Details.** When it bolts from the sanctuary and is cornered on the upper stair, a household guard reaches for his belt, and Alexander acts on instinct first. It weighs him the way Beatrice did, and eases when his hands open. In the Grey Year's winter Thomas finds it in the frozen hedge and brings it in under his coat; it did not make the tracks at the thinning ward, and it will not go near that wall.

**The species.** Humans believe fen boggarts are mischief spirits that sour milk and lead travellers into bogs. In truth they are small, shy, reasoning creatures who live in family groups in undrained fen and water-meadow, and who lead people astray only to keep them from their young. They want wet ground left wet. They communicate in clicks and gesture. Drainage, dogs, and iron traps frighten and injure them. They can consent, and the Harrow's Fen family has consented to Sterling protection, which is one of the unspoken reasons Edward will not sell the Fen. The Vanguard intervenes because enclosure and drainage are destroying their ground. What the characters do about them shows whether land is people to them or only acreage.

### Estate Tenants and Villagers

Mr. Fenwick shouts at a cider cart sunk to the axle and drafts budgets by Michaelmas. Farmer Hodge disputes whether the marrows should be judged by weight or girth, and later reports something at his fences that leaves no track. The Widow Carrow is accused of watering her marrow for eleven years. Davies anchors the tug-of-war team. Together they speak broad Norfolk, cheerful and quarrelsome, and call the heir "Master Alexander." Harrow's Fen, the water-meadow Edward refuses to sell, feeds eleven tenant families.

## Antagonists' People

### Higgins

**Ashcroft mercenary**

**Appearance.** A broad man with a scar through one eyebrow. In Book One he carries a puckered mark beneath his collar where Niall's knife pinned his shoulder in 1845.

**Speech.** Thick-voiced, testing words the way a man tests a step. He is calculating even in pain.

**Details.** He prodded Rhoswen with a pikehead and laughed when she snapped. He calls Niall "the knife-thrower from the cave." Percival calls him "the knife-thrower from Loch Maree." Higgins knows Niall by the wound, Percival by the files. In Book Two he surfaces as an Institute source who can be turned.

### Mr. Ivor Vane

**Hessian's solicitor**

**Appearance.** Narrow and black-clad, with a document case chained to his wrist.

**Speech.** Reverent toward paperwork. "Everything is for sale, Your Grace."

**Details.** He witnesses the deed without understanding what it opens. He is human and unenthralled, simply paid, and knows nothing of what his client is. After Book One his firm keeps the burned tract lawfully in the name of the Count's invented heir and goes on paying its rates.

### Josef

**Hessian's familiar and thrall**

**Appearance.** A thin, colorless young man whose eyes hold nothing of their own.

**Speech.** Only when instructed. He long ago stopped being the kind of man who asked. When Alexander asks his name and whether anyone waits on him at home, he simply repeats his master's terms.

**Details.** Eleven years bound to Hessian by blood dependency, since 1841. He drives the crestless carriage at the festival, unnamed on the box. At noon on the solstice he walks down the shingle path to the bothy, stands outside without knocking, and delivers Hessian's terms. When Hessian comes burning into the cairn, Josef rises out of the dark to meet him. He keeps the cairn after that, feeding the husk slowly on his own blood while he fights his own withdrawal. He is wholly separate from Lucian Voss: Josef is a hollowed servant, and Voss is a thrall who chose surrender and has ambitions of his own.

## Figures in Society and on the Road

### Lady Aldermere and Mrs. Fenhallow

**London society callers**

**Appearance.** Lady Aldermere is tall, jet-beaded, and fond of lorgnettes. Mrs. Fenhallow is small and plump and never still.

**Speech.** Condolence shading into curiosity.

**Details.** They call at the London townhouse in November 1852, the week of Wellington's funeral. Lady Aldermere is old money, mentions the Ashcroft Institute's new lecture season, and delivers the first on-page sneer at the Ashcrofts' insistence on "Lord Silas" and "Lord Percival."

### Josiah Webb

**Northumberland innkeeper at Alwinton**

**Appearance.** Broad, unhurried, with forearms like a blacksmith's.

**Speech.** Flat instruction and the settled patience of a man who has seen every kind of guest.

**Details.** Ewan's grandfather knew him from the kelp trade. His inn at Alwinton burns in the early hours of 6 December 1852, when the Institute comes for the party.

### Tam Rennick

**Drover**

**Appearance.** Red-faced, big, drink-loosened.

**Speech.** Loud Border Scots.

**Details.** Alexander defuses his harassment of an old woman by pitching his voice for the whole room.

### Davey Coop

**Crate-marking boy**

**Appearance.** Twelve, undersized, chalk on his fingers from marking crates.

**Speech.** Dockside quick.

**Details.** His father is dead and his mother takes in washing. Gideon quietly pays for him through the winter.

## Prequel Figures

**Laird Macrae** is the local Highland authority. He corresponds with Edward and receives instructions, and never visits the castle. **Morag Campbell,** a shepherd's daughter, is the first victim, found drowned in the reeds with restraint marks. Her father's pride refuses charity, and Edward calls the money a Crown indemnity. **Iain Macrae,** the Laird's nephew, is the second victim, killed after swearing to shoot the beast for glory; his name is spelled "Iain" throughout the series. **Old Mr. Beaton** heard grinding after midnight and took it for a boat's chains. **The Spectacled Clerk** is slight, his spectacles perpetually sliding down his nose, and he warns that Morag's father will bury his own child.

## Figures from the Past

These people rarely appear on the page, but they shape the principals.

**Elisabeta.** Hessian's human wife. She bore him a daughter, Ilona, in 1480. When he was Turned in 1488 she sent the child to her sister in Hermannstadt and told him the girl had died of fever. His sire, Dragomir, killed her in 1489 to cut Hessian's last human tie. Catherine uncovers the truth in Book Six.

**Ilona.** Elisabeta's daughter by Hessian, raised in Hermannstadt. Her line came west over the centuries, hunted for its blood, and runs through Anton Veress to Amelia.

**Dragomir.** Hessian's sire, a vampire of Vlad Dracula's court whose deeds became the darkest of the Dracula stories. He taught Hessian nothing, killed Elisabeta, and still lives. He enters the story in Book Six.

**Eithne.** The kelpie queen of Loch Maree who co-founded the covenant with Macbeth, King of Scots, and is carved beside him on the relief in the northern refuge. Rhoswen's foremother.

**Anton Veress.** Amelia's true father, the Rowan Circle's scholar, descended from Ilona. He died in the 1815 massacre after binding Seraphina by blood oath to keep his daughter's parentage secret.

**Lady Isobel Marchbank and Lord Marchbank.** Amelia's mother, and the husband whose name gave Amelia legitimacy. He died in the months around her conception.

**Captain Nathaniel Sorrell.** The half-pay officer who led the hunters at the 1815 massacre. Silas valued his spoils.

**The sixteenth Duke of Norwich and Richard Sterling.** The father who initiated both sons without warning on a hunt in 1808, and the heir who died on it.

**Alasdair Fraser.** A Vanguard tracker killed in the autumn of 1838 by a rifle ball meant for Edward. He left behind a compass, a cracked journal, two knives, field notebooks with a coded entry in Gaelic cipher, and the flattened ball Edward kept.

**Agnes Fraser, Isla Fraser, and Janet Lindsay.** Niall's mother refused Edward's money and accepted books, and died in the winter of 1848. His sister Isla, born in the spring of 1839, lives with their widowed aunt Janet Lindsay in Portobello, on Niall's wages.

**The MacDuffs.** Calum's father, Rab, a printing-house porter; his mother, Jean, a laundress; and his younger siblings Kirsty, Wattie, and Ailsa, all still in the Old Town. Niall has sent Jean money anonymously since 1848.

**Samuel and Will Pryce.** The Ashcroft spy who served as the Sterling butler from 1837 until his dismissal in 1841, and his son, Alexander's lost boyhood friend. Will returns in Book Three as one of Percival's agents who remembers Alexander kindly.

**Laurence Vale and the selkie family.** Beatrice's husband, an operative, and the selkie mother and two children who died with him off Stronsay in 1839 while Beatrice waited for authorization.

**Lady Harriet Ashcroft.** Silas's wife, mother of Percival and Margaret, who died in 1839.

## Figures in Later Books

None of these appear in the prequel or Book One.

**Finch,** a gangling, sharp-nosed Vanguard junior who asks too many questions too quickly, joins the Sterling household's operatives in Book Two. **Elara** enters in Book Three: a vampire Turned as a child, ten to twelve in body, with a mind that has kept aging. **Lucian Voss** enters in Book Three: a senior thrall in Hessian's orbit who chose surrender, Calum's dark mirror. **Margaret Ashcroft,** Percival's younger sister, enters in Book Three, floated by her father's allies as a match for Alexander and quietly disaffected from the Institute. **Madam Abigail** enters in Book Three and keeps a London house where brokers and creatures of the hidden world meet on neutral ground. **Finlay MacKenzie** enters in Book Five: a human familiar with long dependency on vampire blood and the human face of its illicit trade. **Dragomir** enters in Book Six. **Vlad Dracula** is never on the page; the truth of his legend surfaces through Dragomir.
